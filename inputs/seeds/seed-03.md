Title: Lay of the Land — capturing a farm's unwritten map before it's lost
One-liner: A retiring farmer drives the property talking into their phone; AI turns GPS track + audio into structured, confidence-tagged map layers that successors can view in AR.
Who it's for: Family farms going through succession. Also golf course superintendents, vineyard managers, large rural estates. Paying business customers: succession advisors, agricultural lenders, rural real estate agents.
The pain it solves: A retiring farmer carries a map that exists nowhere else — drain tiles, the water line to the far trough, buried power cable, the paddock that floods once a decade. Succession researchers note this tacit knowledge has to be demonstrated, not studied. Once the person is gone, finding buried drainage is slow and risky: tile probes and trenching are time-consuming, invasive and can damage the pipes. Best existing tools are paper exercises like Purdue's Code Red Contingency Planning Notebook.
What excites us about it:
- Farmer drives or walks with a phone in their pocket and talks ("Dad put the tile in here in '78, it runs down to the creek"); AI turns GPS track + audio into map layers tagged with year, source and confidence.
- Voice agent follows up later like a curious grandchild ("You mentioned a spring by the north fence. Does it run dry in summer?").
- Successors point their phone at a paddock and see AR overlays ("per Grandad, 1978, medium confidence").
- Shareable dig-safety map for fencers and diggers.
- A documented farm is easier to finance and easier to sell, so lenders and agents have a reason to pay.
