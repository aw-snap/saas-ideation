## Territories

Nine chosen. Nine are computer-centric (the requirement is at least six). They are chosen to be far apart on three things at once: who does the work, what software traps it, and which AI capability unlocks it.

### T1 — Payer-portal grind at small medical practices
- **computer-centric:** yes
- **Scope. In:** eligibility checks, prior-auth submission and status polling, denial follow-up, done by front-desk staff and billers across many payer portals (Availity, NaviNet, each payer's own site). The interim window until payer FHIR APIs land in January 2027 is part of the territory. **Out:** clinical documentation, the patient or family side of appeals (that is T8), phone agents for the front desk (runner-up, not computer-centric), dental and pharmacy system-of-record lock-in (T3).
- **Evidence:** https://www.mgma.com/mgma-stat/how-many-payer-portals-is-too-many-most-practices-already-know-their-answer ; https://www.availity.com/multi-payer-portal/ ; https://caqh.org/hubfs/43908627/drupal/2024-01/2023_CAQH_Index_Report.pdf ; https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/cms-interoperability-prior-authorization-final-rule-cms-0057-f ; https://www.aapc.com/blog/90261-prior-authorization-final-rule-reduces-barriers-to-patient-care/ (screen-work-01, -02). Capability: TC-02, TC-03, TC-07.
- **Why it won:** the best-quantified screen-bound pain in the pool (39 requests per physician per week, 16-24 minutes each, $3.41 manual vs $0.05 electronic, 7-11+ portals per practice), a dated regulatory clock (CMS-0057-F) that raises polling frequency now, and a fit with browser agents that name the job. Thin spot: no first-person quotes from billers (all scouts lacked Reddit access); the 2027 API mandate caps the long-run size, so ideas should aim at what stays manual after the APIs arrive (payer-specific rules, attachments, denials).

### T2 — Invoice intake and the e-invoicing switchover at small firms
- **computer-centric:** yes
- **Scope. In:** fetching invoices and statements from vendor and utility sites, extracting them (scans, PDFs, email), posting into QuickBooks or the ERP, catching extraction errors, and the transition to structured e-invoices under the German, Belgian, French (received from Sept 2026, issued by small firms from Sept 2027) and Spanish mandates. Freight-specific document re-keying is allowed as a sub-niche. **Out:** vendor-fraud checking on payment-change emails (belongs to T5), general bookkeeping, fund accounting for nonprofits (T4 adjacent).
- **Evidence:** https://www.datocms-assets.com/80283/1744404602-ardent-partners-ap-metrics-that-matter-in-2025-pagero-final.pdf ; https://www.docuclipper.com/blog/invoice-data-entry/ ; https://www.g2.com/products/dext/reviews ; https://www.forbes.com/sites/aleksandrabal/2025/11/02/2026-the-year-mandatory-e-invoicing-sweeps-across-europe/ ; https://www.ey.com/en_gl/technical/tax-alerts/french-government-announces-simplification-measures-as-part-of-september-2026-e-invoicing-mandate ; https://mistral.ai/news/mistral-ocr-3/ ; https://github.com/Skyvern-AI/skyvern (screen-work-10, -11; tech-unlocks-03, -22; weak-signals-03). Capability: TC-30, TC-07, TC-25.
- **Why it won:** strong numbers (68% key invoices by hand at $9.84-$15.97 each; 60-300 invoices per clerk per week), a hard dated format change that turns a nice-to-have into a must-do, and a capability cost collapse (OCR at $1-2 per 1,000 pages) that makes the full fetch-parse-post loop demoable in 48 hours. Thin spot: the existing capture tools claim 99% accuracy, so ideas need a wedge (non-standard layouts, sync breakage, the e-invoice format duty, error checking) rather than "OCR again".

### T3 — Locked-in vertical systems of record with API tolls
- **computer-centric:** yes
- **Scope. In:** legacy desktop or hosted practice-management and dealer systems whose vendors charge for or gate API access: dental (Dentrix, Eaglesoft), independent pharmacy (PioneerRx, QS/1), auto dealerships (CDK, Reynolds), veterinary (Cornerstone, Covetrus Pulse), insurance agencies (Applied Epic, AMS360), property management (Yardi, AppFolio). The pain is re-keying between the SoR and everything else, migrations, and vendor lock-in after acquisitions. **Out:** payer portals (T1), invoice capture (T2), consumer-owned PCs, generic RPA infrastructure.

Capability: TC-02, TC-05, TC-06.
- **Why it won:** it is the purest expression of the computer lens ("niche vertical software nobody has modernized") and the same pattern repeats across six verticals, each with dated evidence ($100M CDK settlement, $100-700 per month per location integration tolls, $5,000 per API seat [unverified], Capterra quotes on "waste literal hours"). Thin spot: some fee figures are unverified forum or vendor claims; ideas should pick one vertical and one re-keyed flow, not "connect everything".

## Archive map axes

Three axes. Each idea occupies exactly one bin per axis; the `cell` line in every card names all three.

### Axis 1 — `buyer` (given)
- **B2B:** an organization pays; the user works there. Example: a dental office subscribes.
- **B2C:** an individual pays for their own life. Example: an adult child pays to manage a parent's renewals.
- **prosumer:** an individual pays for their own professional work, without an org procurement step. Example: a solo therapist buys a notes tool.
- **agents:** the paying or primary user is a software agent, or the product exists only because agents are the counterparty. Example: a site charges purchasing agents per call.

### Axis 2 — `track` (given)
- **novel:** depends on a capability from roughly the last 18 months (since about March 2025) or a new interaction paradigm; the core AI loop must really run in the demo.
- **balanced:** uses recent but proven capabilities; the whole demo works end to end in 48 hours.

### Axis 3 — `capability` (defined here)
The capability bin is the one thing the AI does that, if removed, makes the pitch collapse. If two bins seem to apply, take the one listed first. This precedence rule is what keeps the bins non-overlapping.

1. **agent-infra:** the AI's counterparty is another agent: identity, payments, access control, tool supply, or verification of agent runs. Example: a paywall that admits verified purchasing agents and charges them per request.
2. **local-private:** inference runs on the buyer's own device or premises, and that is the reason to buy. Example: session notes drafted by a model on the therapist's laptop, nothing leaving the machine.
3. **screen-agent:** the AI operates a browser, desktop or mobile UI that has no usable API. Example: an agent that logs into seven payer portals and polls prior-auth status.
4. **verifier:** the AI checks, scores or flags someone else's output (human or AI) against a source of truth, and does not itself act. Example: every citation in a draft brief checked against the case database before filing.
5. **extractor:** the AI turns unstructured input (scans, PDFs, images, video, audio) into structured records. Example: a pile of scanned invoices becomes ledger entries with line items.
6. **drafter-dialogue:** the AI produces new text, media or spoken turns for a human: drafting, summarizing, translating, or holding a live conversation. Example: a voice agent answering a clinic's phone; a tool that rewrites one dataset into each funder's report format.

## Runners-up
- **Language access and high-stakes translation for low-resource languages** (overlooked-14, -15, -16): strong evidence (655-evaluation study, KFF), but the core work is live interpretation and forms mixed together, and the buyer is unclear after the EO 13166 rollback. Best B2C candidate not chosen.
- **Accessibility remediation deadlines** (weak-signals-04, -12; overlooked-24): strong dated mandates (ADA Title II to 2028, EAA live), but remediation tooling is a well-served market and the work is testing, not a new AI loop.
- **MCP tool supply chain security and auth** (tech-unlocks-08, -09, -10): named CVEs and an audited registry, but the buyer is developers and the space is moving fast; folded under T6's "out" list.
- **Agentic checkout for small merchants on ACP, AP2 and card-network tokens** (tech-unlocks-11; weak-signals-26, -27): strong dated launches, but protocol plumbing is being built by Stripe, Shopify and Google themselves; kept out of T6 so T6 stays about access.
- **Freight brokerage document re-keying and legacy TMS** (screen-work-08, -09; overlooked-12): strong numbers from one vendor blog; reachable as a sub-niche inside T2 or T3.
- **New recurring filings for micro-sellers and sole traders** (weak-signals-01, -02, -11; overlooked-18): UK MTD (864k people from Apr 2026) and per-shipment customs after de minimis are strong, but the shape duplicates T4 and the software layer (MTD-approved tools, brokers) is already forming.
- **Mandated-but-messy public data** (weak-signals-14, -15, -16): hospital price files, EU high-value datasets, DPP registry; real but the buyer (brokers, researchers, agents) is diffuse and no pain quotes exist yet.
- **Gig-worker deactivation appeals** (overlooked-26): 250k-440k events a year and an EU directive due Dec 2026, but the counterparty is a platform that controls the process; hard to demo a working loop.
- **Missed calls at dental and clinic front desks** (tech-unlocks-24): not computer-centric, and the cartographer notes EHR-syncing voice vendors already exist.
- **Private control of native desktop apps, on-premises transcription, voice-agent tuning, long-video review, shelf and site counting** (tech-unlocks-05, -25, -26, -27, -28): overlaps the seed lane.
