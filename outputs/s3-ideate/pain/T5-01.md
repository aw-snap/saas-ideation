# T5-01, proving security to outsiders: insurance, CMMC, HIPAA

1. **Who:** small-firm owner/office manager, no IT staff | **Regime:** cyber-insurance renewal | **What hurts:** questionnaires ballooned in length and now demand documented proof, not attestation | **How often:** annual renewal | **Cost:** hours per renewal, unspecified | **Workaround:** owner fills it out alone, hunting through consoles | **Evidence:** "Her cyber insurance renewal questionnaire was three times longer than last year's" and "questionnaires that used to take fifteen minutes now run sixty to a hundred and fifty questions"; one owner said "I don't even know what half of these are asking." Source: https://www.snl-techservices.com/post/cyber-insurance-readiness-small-business (2025/2026) | **Severity:** 4 — recurring, growing burden with no in-house expertise to meet it.

2. **Who:** small manufacturer/electronics firm (ICS, Decatur IL) | **Regime:** cyber-insurance application | **What hurts:** insurer sought to rescind the policy and deny a ransomware claim over an MFA answer | **How often:** one-time but exemplifies a pattern | **Cost:** loss of coverage and denial of duty to defend/indemnify a ransomware claim | **Workaround:** none — company signed the application believing MFA was in place | **Evidence:** Travelers alleged ICS's application, signed by the CEO and the person "responsible for the applicant's network and information security," stated the company used MFA for administrative/privileged access when it allegedly did not; Travelers sought to have the court "declare the insurance contract null and void." Source: https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/national/2022/07/12/675516.htm (2022, still cited in 2026 commentary at https://www.itsasap.com/blog/travelers-vs-ics) | **Severity:** 5 — total loss of coverage after a real breach, tied directly to a security-attestation answer.

3. **Who:** small businesses generally, cyber-insurance buyers | **Regime:** claims/underwriting | **What hurts:** misrepresentation on applications is a common, material basis for denial | **How often:** ongoing across the industry | **Cost:** roughly 10% of insurance claims denied annually for misrepresentation, per general industry figures cited alongside the case coverage | **Workaround:** declare gaps with a remediation date rather than answer optimistically | **Evidence:** "approximately 10% of insurance claims are denied annually due to misrepresentation." Source: https://www.nextinsurance.com/business-insurance/edu/misrepresentation-in-insurance/ (2025/2026) | **Severity:** 3 — general base rate, not small-firm-specific, but shows the stakes of the questionnaire.

4. **Who:** small businesses without MFA fully deployed | **Regime:** cyber-insurance underwriting/claims | **What hurts:** partial MFA deployment (e.g., on the firewall but not the server environment, or on RDP but not M365/domain admin) reads as a flat "no" on the form even when the business believes it qualifies "yes" | **How often:** every renewal cycle | **Cost:** claim denial risk after breach | **Workaround:** manually audit every admin surface before answering | **Evidence:** "A firm can have MFA locked down on remote desktop access while leaving Microsoft 365 logins and domain admin accounts wide open, and that's a 'no' on the questionnaire even though the business believes it qualifies for a 'yes.'" Source: search-derived summary of cyber-insurance renewal commentary, 2026, https://gogravity.net/blog/cyber-insurance-renewal-questionnaire-walkthrough-2026/ | **Severity:** 4 — the gap between believed and actual compliance is exactly where claims get denied.

5. **Who:** cyber-insurance underwriters/claims data (Coalition) | **Regime:** cyber-insurance | **What hurts:** MFA gaps are the dominant driver of the losses carriers are trying to screen for with these questionnaires | **How often:** ongoing, per claims data | **Cost:** n/a (aggregate stat) | **Workaround:** none named | **Evidence:** "82% of claims involved organizations without MFA properly in place," per Coalition's 2024 Cyber Threat Index, cited in a small-business readiness post. Source: https://www.snl-techservices.com/post/cyber-insurance-readiness-small-business (2025/2026) | **Severity:** 3 — explains why the questionnaire burden exists, not itself a first-person complaint.

6. **Who:** small DoD subcontractors (5-50 employees), often manufacturers | **Regime:** CMMC Level 2 self-assessment / C3PAO third-party audit | **What hurts:** total compliance cost far exceeds the audit fee alone, and manufacturers accustomed to verbal processes must produce "comprehensive written documentation of their security practices" | **How often:** once to prepare, then annual affirmation, triennial reassessment | **Cost:** C3PAO fees "typically start at $40,000 and can exceed $80,000," DoD's own estimate for a Level 2 third-party assessment is $104,670 total (assessment $76,743 + prep $20,699 + reporting $2,851 + affirmations $4,377), and total compliance investment "typically ranging from $50,000 to $300,000+" | **Workaround:** consultants, MSPs, readiness tools | **Evidence:** figures above. Source: https://www.huntress.com/cmmc-compliance-guide/cmmc-certification-cost and https://elevateconsult.com/insights/cmmc-certification-cost-breakdown-hidden-level-2-expenses-defense-contractors-miss-in-2026/ (2026) | **Severity:** 5 — dollar figures this large relative to a small subcontractor's revenue can decide whether they stay in the defense supply chain.

7. **Who:** small manufacturer subcontractors with legacy shop-floor equipment | **Regime:** CMMC / NIST SP 800-171 | **What hurts:** "Many manufacturers operate equipment with outdated operating systems that cannot easily implement modern security controls," forcing isolation workarounds; full C3PAO prep takes "6-12 months" | **How often:** one-time buildout, then maintained continuously | **Cost:** not quantified beyond the 6-12 month timeline | **Workaround:** network segmentation/isolation of legacy equipment | **Evidence:** as quoted. Source: https://godlan.com/cmmc-2-0-deadlines-rules/ (Gate B source) | **Severity:** 4 — legacy equipment on a shop floor cannot simply be patched, so the fix cost is structural, not procedural.

8. **Who:** DoD, and by extension the small-business supply base | **Regime:** CMMC Phase 2 rollout | **What hurts:** the program itself was destabilized — DoD suspended Phase 2 C3PAO third-party assessment requirements on 13 July 2026 pending a 60-day review, after receiving roughly 1,100 comment responses (11,000+ pages), more than half supportive of reform | **How often:** ongoing policy uncertainty | **Cost:** sunk readiness spending by firms that had already begun C3PAO prep for the (now paused) November 2026 deadline | **Workaround:** none — firms are left not knowing which requirements will stick | **Evidence:** "The DoW paused the Phase 2 transition to third-party assessments and opened a 60-day review of the program on July 13, 2026," and it drew "roughly 1,100 responses amounting to more than 11,000 pages of feedback." Source: https://federalnewsnetwork.com/cybersecurity/2026/07/pentagon-suspends-cmmc-phase-two-requirements-launches-review-of-program/ and https://secureframe.com/blog/cmmc-news-2026-phase-2-pause (July-Sept 2026) | **Severity:** 4 — moving-target compliance regime makes any spending or planning by a small firm feel wasted.

9. **Who:** small defense subcontractor (Swiss Automation Inc., Illinois precision machining, ~5-50 employees) | **Regime:** DFARS 252.204-7012 / NIST SP 800-171, False Claims Act | **What hurts:** DOJ's first FCA settlement targeting the defense supply chain for failing to provide "adequate cybersecurity" for technical drawings supplied to DoD prime contractors, triggered by a whistleblower (a former quality-control manager) | **How often:** one-time enforcement action, but signals ongoing DOJ focus | **Cost:** company paid $421,234 to resolve the claims; the whistleblower received $65,291 | **Workaround:** none — discovered only via qui tam suit | **Evidence:** company "knowing[ly] fail[ed] to provide adequate cybersecurity, as required by DFARS 252.204-7012, for the technical drawings of certain parts that [the company] supplied to contractors." Source: https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/illinois-precision-machining-company-agrees-pay-421234-resolve-alleged-false-claims (December 2025) | **Severity:** 5 — a small subcontractor with no dedicated compliance staff faced six-figure FCA liability plus legal exposure from an internal whistleblower.

10. **Who:** small defense contractor in Alabama | **Regime:** DFARS/NIST SP 800-171, False Claims Act | **What hurts:** similar cybersecurity-related FCA settlement | **How often:** one-time | **Cost:** $507,144 paid to resolve liability | **Workaround:** none named | **Evidence:** "Alabama Defense Contractor Agrees to Pay $507,144 to Resolve False Claims Act Liability Relating to Cybersecurity Violations." Source: https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/alabama-defense-contractor-agrees-pay-507144-resolve-false-claims-act-liability-relating (2025) | **Severity:** 5 — confirms the Illinois case is part of a pattern hitting small contractors, not an outlier.

11. **Who:** small covered entities (medical/dental practices, EMS providers, ambulatory surgery centers) | **Regime:** HIPAA Security Rule, OCR Risk Analysis Initiative | **What hurts:** OCR's active enforcement initiative (launched October 2024) is finding that small entities never conducted, or never updated, a required security risk analysis; "in every case, OCR found the practice had no written Risk Analysis or had one that did not reflect current systems" | **How often:** OCR reached 13 settlements under this initiative by April 2026 | **Cost:** Bryan County EMS paid $90,000 (Oct 2024); Deer Oaks paid $225,000 (Jul 2025); Northeast Radiology paid $350,000 (Apr 2025); Syracuse ASC paid $250,000 (Jul 2025) | **Workaround:** none — these entities had not done the risk analysis at all | **Evidence:** figures and quote above. Source: search-derived summary citing https://ogletree.com/insights-resources/blog-posts/2025-enforcement-trends-risk-analysis-failures-at-the-center-of-hhss-multimillion-dollar-hipaa-penalties/ (2025/2026) | **Severity:** 5 — six-figure penalties for a paperwork gap, hitting entities with no compliance staff.

12. **Who:** OCR / small covered entities overall | **Regime:** HIPAA enforcement | **What hurts:** risk-analysis failure is "the most commonly identified HIPAA Security Rule violation in OCR's investigations of data breaches and audits," and OCR closed 2025 with 21 settlements/penalties, its second-highest annual total | **How often:** ongoing, year over year | **Cost:** n/a aggregate | **Workaround:** none named | **Evidence:** as quoted. Source: https://ogletree.com/insights-resources/blog-posts/2025-enforcement-trends-risk-analysis-failures-at-the-center-of-hhss-multimillion-dollar-hipaa-penalties/ (2025) | **Severity:** 3 — context stat, not a first-person account, but shows the exposure is systemic, not rare.

13. **Who:** solo practitioners and small single-site practices | **Regime:** HIPAA Security Rule risk analysis | **What hurts:** the free HHS SRA Tool is a 156-question branching assessment; sources state that "most organizations quickly outgrow the SRA Tool" once their environment is anything but a single site | **How often:** required at least annually to stay current, per OCR guidance emphasis | **Cost:** not quantified beyond tool cost (free) but implies added consultant/paid-tool spend after outgrowing it | **Workaround:** free HHS SRA Tool first, then a paid platform | **Evidence:** "the 156 question app," and "most organizations quickly outgrow the SRA Tool and upgrade to a platform ... for guided assessments." Source: https://medcurity.com/best-hipaa-risk-assessment-tools/ (Sept 2025 update referenced) | **Severity:** 3 — tool limitation forces a second purchase for anything beyond the smallest practice. `[unverified]` beyond what is quoted; no direct user complaint found.

14. **Who:** all HIPAA-covered small practices | **Regime:** proposed HIPAA Security Rule overhaul (NPRM, Jan 6 2025) | **What hurts:** the rule is not yet finalized as of September 2026 ("the comment period closing March 7, 2025 ... No final rule as of 2026, and the current Security Rule stays in effect in the meantime"), leaving practices unable to plan, while the draft would add mandatory MFA, encryption at rest/in transit, elimination of "addressable" (i.e., optional) safeguards, and annual risk assessments with detailed documentation, with only ~180 days to comply once finalized | **How often:** would become a one-time compliance overhaul, then ongoing annual requirement | **Cost:** regulatory impact analysis estimated $9 billion in first-year compliance costs across all covered entities and business associates, with ~$6 billion in recurring annual costs; the article notes "the costs would disproportionately burden small and rural providers already operating on thin margins" | **Workaround:** none — practices are waiting on a final rule | **Evidence:** figures and quotes above. Source: https://livecompliance.com/blog/2026-hipaa-security-rule-overhaul/ (2026) | **Severity:** 4 — large looming cost with an aggressive compliance window and no finalized rule to plan against yet.

## Incumbents
- **HHS SRA Tool** (free, HHS/ONC) — v3.6 released Sept 2025, a 156-question branching desktop/iPad app; sources say it suits only "a very small, single-site practice" and that "most organizations quickly outgrow" it. https://medcurity.com/best-hipaa-risk-assessment-tools/
- **PreVeil** — CMMC/NIST 800-171 readiness and encrypted-communication tool for small defense contractors, cited for cost-saving guidance around CMMC certification. https://www.preveil.com/blog/6-ways-to-save-money-cmmc-costs/
- **Coalition, At-Bay, Cowbell** — SMB-focused cyber insurers; Cowbell is described as "purpose-built for small and mid-sized businesses" with "simpler processes ... for buyers who are not insurance sophisticates," while Coalition's active-monitoring platform "creates a higher-engagement relationship than some small businesses want." https://seedpodcyber.com/cyber-insurance-carrier-comparison/
- **C3PAO third-party assessors** — required for CMMC Level 2 certification; fees "typically start at $40,000 and can exceed $80,000" per assessment, on top of remediation costs, and the requirement itself was suspended by DoD in July 2026 pending review. https://www.huntress.com/cmmc-compliance-guide/cmmc-certification-cost
- **Compliancy Group, Medcurity** — paid HIPAA compliance platforms small practices move to after outgrowing the free HHS SRA Tool; specific user-review complaints were not found in this pass.
