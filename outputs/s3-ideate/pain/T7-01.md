# T7-01 — Legal: AI-generated court filings and citations (verifying/triaging)

1. **Who**: Federal judges (EDNY, Judge Lee Dunst) / **Setting**: routine civil motion / **What hurts**: forced to research attorney-misconduct case law instead of the merits / **How often**: per incident found (5 nonexistent cases in one motion) / **Cost**: judge's own time diverted / **Workaround**: manual survey of sanctions case law / **Evidence**: judge said he had "no choice but to survey the case law regarding attorney misconduct relating to the use of AI" rather than "resolving a routine matter of civil procedure," April 2025. Source: https://news.bloomberglaw.com/legal-ops-and-tech/ai-faked-cases-become-core-issue-irritating-overworked-judges — **Severity 4** (chronic drain on already-scarce judicial time).

2. **Who**: Judge Marina Garcia Marmolejo, S.D. Tex., Laredo Division / **Setting**: busy border-district docket / **What hurts**: no staff capacity to hunt down fake AI citations / **How often**: ongoing, docket-wide / **Cost**: unquantified time, described as one of the busiest dockets in the nation / **Workaround**: none noted / **Evidence**: "there are scant resources to spare ferreting out erroneous AI citations in the first place, let alone surveying the burgeoning caselaw on this subject," July 2025. Source: https://news.bloomberglaw.com/legal-ops-and-tech/ai-faked-cases-become-core-issue-irritating-overworked-judges — **Severity 4** (resource-constrained courts hit hardest).

3. **Who**: Two attorneys, Coomer v. Lindell / **Setting**: federal court, Colorado / **What hurts**: brief with nearly 30 defective citations submitted and had to be litigated over / **How often**: single filing, ~30 errors / **Cost**: $3,000 fine each, July 2025 / **Workaround**: after-the-fact sanctions briefing / **Evidence**: "nearly 30 defective citations," fined $3,000 each, July 2025. Source: https://www.lawnext.com/2025/05/ai-hallucinations-strike-again-two-more-cases-where-lawyers-face-judicial-wrath-for-fake-citations.html — **Severity 4** (large error volume in one document, real money).

4. **Who**: Illinois law firm and partner / **Setting**: federal litigation, opposing counsel discovered fabrications / **What hurts**: opposing counsel forced to spend billable time contesting an AI-tainted motion / **How often**: single filing / **Cost**: ordered to pay $59,500 combined to opposing firm / **Workaround**: opposing counsel's own manual detection and fee petition / **Evidence**: "ordered to pay a combined $59,500 to the opposition law firm that discovered fake citations," 2025. Source: https://news.bloomberglaw.com/legal-ops-and-tech/ai-fake-citations-expose-lawyer-sloppiness-and-training-gaps — **Severity 5** (large, quantified, directly tied to detection labor).

5. **Who**: Law firm (14th-largest U.S. firm involved) / **Setting**: discovery briefing struck by special master / **What hurts**: entire briefs thrown out, fees shifted / **How often**: single matter, multiple briefs struck / **Cost**: $31,100 ordered paid in defendant's legal fees / **Workaround**: special master review / **Evidence**: "ordered to jointly pay $31,100 in defendant's legal fees," 2025. Source: https://tagteam.harvard.edu/hub_feeds/3622/feed_items/13788105/content — **Severity 4**.

6. **Who**: Steven A. Schwartz and Peter LoDuca, Mata v. Avianca / **Setting**: S.D.N.Y. / **What hurts**: six fabricated cases in an opposition brief, required personal letters to every falsely-cited judge / **How often**: single filing, six fake cases / **Cost**: $5,000 Rule 11 sanction, June 2023, plus reputational fallout that became the field's reference case / **Workaround**: none; discovered by opposing counsel and the court / **Evidence**: "$5,000 joint-and-several Rule 11 sanction; letters to plaintiff and to each falsely-named judge were required," June 22, 2023. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mata_v._Avianca,_Inc. — **Severity 5** (founding precedent, still cited as the baseline cost).

7. **Who**: Litigators and courts relying on paid legal-AI research tools / **Setting**: any brief drafted with Lexis+ AI, Westlaw AI-Assisted Research, or general LLMs / **What hurts**: the incumbent "hallucination-free" tools still fabricate at meaningful rates, so verification can't be skipped even with paid tools / **How often**: measured across benchmark queries / **Cost**: not monetized, but implies mandatory manual re-check of AI-tool output / **Workaround**: manual cite-checking regardless of tool used / **Evidence**: "hallucination rates were 17% for Lexis+ AI, 33% for Westlaw AI-Assisted Research, and 43% for GPT-4," Stanford RegLab study. Source: https://reglab.stanford.edu/publications/hallucination-free-assessing-the-reliability-of-leading-ai-legal-research-tools/ — **Severity 5** (undercuts the incumbent tools' core marketing claim).

8. **Who**: Self-represented (pro se) litigants and the clerks/staff attorneys who process their filings / **Setting**: any court accepting pro se filings / **What hurts**: pro se filers use AI because they can't afford counsel, then file fabricated authority that clerks and staff attorneys must catch / **How often**: majority share of documented cases / **Cost**: unquantified added screening time / **Workaround**: none — court instructions to pro se filers "do not contain any mentions of AI usage" / **Evidence**: "59% of all documented hallucination cases involve pro se litigants," 2026 tracker analysis. Source: https://www.haqq.ai/blog/ai-legal-hallucination-audit — **Severity 4** (large volume, least-resourced parties on both sides).

9. **Who**: Pro Se Staff Attorneys / U.S. district courts / **Setting**: screening in forma pauperis and sua sponte dockets / **What hurts**: existing statutory screening duty now includes chasing fabricated authority with no updated guidance / **How often**: ongoing caseload duty / **Cost**: not stated / **Workaround**: ad hoc; court instructions unchanged / **Evidence**: "Courts already provide instructions to pro se filers, but they do not contain any mentions of AI usage, the phenomenon of hallucinations, or potential sanctions for hallucinated citations," 2026. Source: https://blog.citp.princeton.edu/2026/05/27/can-ai-reduce-burdens-on-courts-by-automatically-verifying-citations/ — **Severity 3** (procedural gap, not yet quantified in cost).

10. **Who**: Tracking researchers / whole U.S. and international court system / **Setting**: cumulative case count / **What hurts**: volume of hallucination incidents is accelerating, not plateauing / **How often**: from ~200 cases mid-2025 to 1,598 by June 9, 2026 / **Cost**: n/a (volume metric) / **Workaround**: independent tracker (Charlotin database), not a court tool / **Evidence**: "roughly 200 cases in mid-2025, 719 by January 2026, 1,227 by early April 2026, 1,598 by June 9, 2026." Source: https://www.damiencharlotin.com/hallucinations/ — **Severity 5** (documents the trend is worsening, not self-correcting).

11. **Who**: Litigants and filers subject to judge-specific standing orders / **Setting**: any court with a GenAI standing order / **What hurts**: rules "vary dramatically across jurisdictions, leaving many practitioners uncertain about their obligations"; some require disclosure of AI tool name, others require certification of verification, inconsistently defined / **How often**: per filing, per judge / **Cost**: added compliance research per case, unquantified / **Workaround**: private trackers of standing orders (law-firm client alerts) / **Evidence**: "Given the speed with which judges are issuing these orders, there has been a lack of consistency, which only adds to confusion and imposes additional burdens and costs on litigants," 2026. Source: https://library.law.unc.edu/2026/02/judicial-guidance-on-the-use-of-genai-in-court/ — **Severity 4** (compliance burden multiplies with every new judge encountered).

12. **Who**: Opposing counsel / **Setting**: California Court of Appeal, Noland v. Land of the Free / **What hurts**: court declined to award opposing counsel fees for a frivolous AI-tainted appeal because opposing counsel itself failed to flag the fabricated citations — shifting a verification duty onto the other side with no compensation if missed / **How often**: single ruling but cited as a new standard / **Cost**: lost fee award for opposing counsel due to their own non-detection / **Workaround**: none yet; commentary calls for heightened vigilance / **Evidence**: "the court declined to award attorney fees to opposing counsel...noting that respondents did not alert the court to the fabricated citations." Source: https://wisblawg.law.wisc.edu/2025/09/16/does-fee-denial-signal-new-expectations-for-detecting-opposing-counsels-ai-hallucinations/, September 2025 — **Severity 4** (creates new unfunded duty to catch the other side's fabrications).

13. **Who**: Litigators reviewing their own or a colleague's AI-assisted draft, and paralegals doing manual cite-checking / **Setting**: pre-filing internal review / **What hurts**: full manual cite-check of a brief is slow / **How often**: per brief / **Cost**: "Manual cite-checking typically takes 2-5 hours per brief depending on complexity and number of citations" (vendor-stated baseline) / **Workaround**: Bluebook manual pull of every case / **Evidence**: 2-5 hours per brief manual baseline, 2026. Source: https://attorneyatlawmagazine.com/legal-technology/ai/the-vanishing-billable-and-non-billable-hour-part-2-clearbrief — **Severity 3** (vendor-reported figure, directionally consistent with other reports; not independently verified) [unverified].

14. **Who**: Legal proofreaders / cite-checkers (contract and staff roles) / **Setting**: law firms and legal process outsourcers / **What hurts**: dedicated, repetitive lower-wage role exists specifically to verify names, dates, citations, and legal language before filing / **How often**: ongoing job function / **Cost**: "average hourly pay for a Legal Proofreader job in the US is $27.65," range $20.43-$33.65/hr, 2026 / **Workaround**: this role itself is the workaround / **Evidence**: hourly wage figures, ZipRecruiter, 2026. Source: https://www.ziprecruiter.com/Salaries/Legal-Proofreader-Salary — **Severity 3** (shows the work is routinized and outsourced, but not AI-specific).

15. **Who**: Attorneys firm-wide at Morgan & Morgan (1,000+ lawyers) / **Setting**: internal firm risk management after a Wyoming federal judge threatened sanctions / **What hurts**: firm had to send an urgent, firm-wide compliance warning after fabricated case law appeared in a filing against Walmart / **How often**: triggered by one incident, applied to entire firm / **Cost**: reputational, plus threat of termination stated in the memo / **Workaround**: internal warning email, not a systemic check / **Evidence**: "sent an urgent email to its more than 1,000 lawyers warning that using AI-generated fake case law in court filings could get them fired," 2025. Source: https://www.fastcompany.com/91280650/ai-hallucinations-could-get-lawyers-fired-law-firm — **Severity 4** (firm-wide operational and reputational exposure from a single lawyer's unchecked draft).

16. **Who**: Associate at a Colorado firm / **Setting**: sanctions proceeding / **What hurts**: individual career consequence — associate initially blamed a legal intern, then admitted ChatGPT use, and was reportedly fired / **How often**: single documented instance / **Cost**: job loss / **Workaround**: none; discovered through sanctions inquiry / **Evidence**: "the lawyer was later fired from his law firm" after admitting ChatGPT-generated citations. Source: https://spellbook.com/learn/lawyer-fined-using-ai-legal-fake-citations — **Severity 4**.

17. **Who**: Stanford's Riana Pfefferkorn, commenting on the field / **Setting**: overall trend commentary / **What hurts**: problem is outgrowing ad hoc handling by individual judges / **How often**: systemic / **Cost**: not monetized / **Workaround**: none yet; individual judges handling case-by-case / **Evidence**: "It just is metastasizing in size...this is something that is actually becoming a widespread enough nuisance that it will merit treatment as a core problem." Source: https://news.bloomberglaw.com/legal-ops-and-tech/ai-faked-cases-become-core-issue-irritating-overworked-judges — **Severity 4**.

18. **Who**: Lawyers reviewing opposing filings / **Setting**: a case where a lawyer was sanctioned specifically for failing to catch the *other side's* fabricated citations rather than for creating them / **What hurts**: a new duty of diligence is emerging that requires actively cite-checking the opponent's authority, not just one's own / **How often**: single documented ruling, described as "a new wrinkle" / **Cost**: sanction imposed on the non-drafting lawyer / **Workaround**: none established / **Evidence**: headline and reporting, "Lawyers Dinged for Failing to Detect Opponent's Fake Citations," September 2025. Source: https://www.lawnext.com/2025/09/a-new-wrinkle-in-ai-hallucination-cases-lawyers-dinged-for-failing-to-detect-opponents-fake-citations.html — **Severity 4** (expands who is on the hook for verification, doubling the checking burden per filing).

## Incumbents
- **Westlaw AI-Assisted Research / KeyCite** — Stanford RegLab found a 33% hallucination rate despite the product's accuracy claims. https://reglab.stanford.edu/publications/hallucination-free-assessing-the-reliability-of-leading-ai-legal-research-tools/
- **Lexis+ AI / Shepard's** — same Stanford study found a 17% hallucination rate, still requiring independent verification. https://reglab.stanford.edu/publications/hallucination-free-assessing-the-reliability-of-leading-ai-legal-research-tools/
- **Clearbrief** — vendor claims manual cite-checking (2-5 hrs/brief) drops to 15-30 minutes and saves ~7 hours/week of litigation work; user reviews on G2 note the value of citation/fact verification (4.4 rating, 9 reviews) but this is vendor- and small-sample-sourced. https://attorneyatlawmagazine.com/legal-technology/ai/the-vanishing-billable-and-non-billable-hour-part-2-clearbrief [unverified re: independent replication]
- **CoCounsel** — reported to integrate with Westlaw for research but described as lacking the same depth of brief-level citation verification as specialized tools. https://aitoolsbakery.com/blog/best-ai-tools-for-paralegals/
- **General-purpose LLMs (ChatGPT, GPT-4)** — highest hallucination rate measured, 43% in the Stanford benchmark, yet still the tool most commonly implicated in sanctions cases (Mata v. Avianca and successors). https://reglab.stanford.edu/publications/hallucination-free-assessing-the-reliability-of-leading-ai-legal-research-tools/
