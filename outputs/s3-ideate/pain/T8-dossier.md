# T8 dossier: proxy admin for an aging parent

Sources: `outputs/s3-ideate/pain/T8-01.md` (health coverage) and `outputs/s3-ideate/pain/T8-02.md` (money and estate). Neither miner could reach Reddit or other forums, so the evidence is mostly primary or aggregate (KFF, CBPP, CFPB, FBI IC3, SSA OIG, CRS). Verbatim quotes from proxies are scarce.

## Pain points

**P1. Medicaid renewals lost on paperwork, not eligibility.**
- Who: adult children and guardians handling a parent's Medicaid renewal.
- What hurts: most unwinding disenrollments were procedural. Long-term-care recipients typically get 30 days to answer a renewal packet, often mailed to the parent rather than the proxy.
- How often: yearly per enrollee. 69% of disenrollments were procedural (Sept 2024).
- Cost: loss of coverage, including nursing-home payment. The sources give no dollar figure or gap length.
- Workaround: reinstatement within 90 days (some states), or reapplying.
- Evidence: "nearly seven in ten (69%) were disenrolled for paperwork or procedural reasons" (https://www.cbpp.org/research/health/unwinding-watch-tracking-medicaid-coverage-as-pandemic-protections-end) ; 30-day window (https://www.medicaid.gov/sites/default/files/2023-12/considerations-for-procedural-termination-strategies.pdf). State data is unreliable: Iowa's rate was corrected from 1% to 29%.
- Severity: 5

**P2. Medicare Advantage denials go unappealed although appeals usually win.**
- Who: family members managing a parent's MA plan.
- What hurts: denials arrive mid-crisis, and few families appeal.
- How often: 2024: 4.1M of 52.8M prior-auth requests denied (7.7%). Only 11.5% appealed, and 80.7% of appeals overturned the denial.
- Cost: care goes without, or the family pays $300–$600 per Level 1/2 appeal for a patient advocate, which insurance "very rarely" covers (vendor figure, https://understoodcare.com/care-guides/what-a-patient-advocate-costs-for-a-single-medicare-appeal-vs-an-ongoing-engagement/).
- Workaround: DIY appeal within 65 days (a 72-hour expedited route exists), a paid advocate, or giving up.
- Evidence: https://www.kff.org/medicare/medicare-advantage-insurers-made-nearly-53-million-prior-authorization-determinations-in-2024/ ; one daughter found "his plan had denied additional days at a skilled nursing facility, the same week his doctor was recommending he stay" (https://senioridy.com/medicare-advantage-prior-authorization-denial/).
- Severity: 5

**P3. Banks and agencies refuse to recognise the proxy.**
- Who: POA agents and would-be Medicare representatives.
- What hurts: banks demand their own POA form or a physician letter, or require a housebound owner to appear in person. CMS-1696 requires proof of legal authority, and CMS "reserves the right to request documentation" at any time.
- How often: common enough that the CFPB keeps standing answer pages on it. No national rejection rate was found.
- Cost: unpaid bills for months. One 94-year-old "went without her pension money for seven months" (https://amp.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.1210222). Attorney fees to intervene.
- Workaround: presenting the POA early, attorney demand letters, switching banks.
- Evidence: "I was told the POA has to be on the bank/credit union's form. What can I do?" (https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/my-family-member-signed-a-power-of-attorney-poa-but-when-i-took-it-to-the-bankcredit-union-i-was-told-the-poa-has-to-be-on-the-bankcredit-unions-form-what-can-i-do-en-1151/) ; https://www.cms.gov/files/document/li-net-appointment-representative-form.pdf
- Severity: 5

**P4. Elder fraud is found only after the money is gone.**
- Who: adult children watching a parent's accounts.
- What hurts: gift-card, romance and investment scams move money instantly and irreversibly. Families notice weeks or months later.
- How often: 147,127 IC3 complaints in 2024, up 46%.
- Cost: $4.885B lost in 2024. 7,500 victims lost over $100k each.
- Workaround: EverSafe or Carefull monitoring, vigilance, reporting after the fact.
- Evidence: https://www.aarp.org/money/scams-fraud/fbi-report-fraud-2024/ ; "store surveillance footage is months gone and retailers have no records of who used the cards" (https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2023/08/if-you-paid-scammer-gift-card-your-money-gone-maybe-not)
- Severity: 5

**P5. State Medicaid and plan portals fail at login and at support.**
- Who: proxies checking a parent's status or documents.
- What hurts: login errors, and secure-message replies that just say "contact the insurance provider". The failure comes before any question of delegated access.
- How often: a recurring theme in reviews of the FL, TX and Centene apps. No count was found.
- Cost: "wasting days trying to log in".
- Workaround: phone calls.
- Evidence: https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=us.tx.state.hhsc.ytbmobile&hl=en_US ; https://apps.apple.com/us/app/fl-medicaid-member-portal/id1532966588 (paraphrased review)
- Severity: 4

**P6. Fiduciary record-keeping and audits.**
- Who: SSA representative payees, VA fiduciaries, informal POA agents.
- What hurts: VA fiduciaries handling more than $10k a year file annual accountings and report every change in the veteran's circumstances. SSA OIG audits whether payees "used and accounted for" benefits. POA agents are told to "document everything" or risk abuse accusations.
- How often: annual accountings, plus unpredictable audits.
- Cost: legal exposure. Programme-wide, 167 VA misuse cases led to $618k reissued in one year.
- Workaround: manual books, spreadsheets.
- Evidence: https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF13019 ; https://oig-files.ssa.gov/audits/full/A-05-18-50587.pdf
- Severity: 4

**P7. Monthly bill-pay and account watching.**
- Who: adult children acting as informal money managers.
- What hurts: checking several accounts for missed payments, late fees, duplicate charges and zombie subscriptions.
- How often: monthly. Professionals estimate at least about 4 hours a month.
- Cost: $25–$100/hour or $130/month for a daily money manager (DMM), or the family's unpaid time.
- Workaround: DMMs, bank alerts, spreadsheets, volunteer bill-payers through Area Agencies on Aging.
- Evidence: "Most clients need approximately four hours of services per month" (https://www.elderlawanswers.com/daily-money-managers-can-help-assist-seniors-with-financial-matters-9167)
- Severity: 3

**P8. The wrong account relationship causes problems years later.**
- Who: adult children, set up by bank staff.
- What hurts: the child is added as a joint owner when a convenience signer would fit. That creates Medicaid asset exposure and disputes after death.
- How often: "In most cases, banks... add an individual to an account as a joint owner."
- Cost: Medicaid disqualification until the balance is spent down.
- Workaround: seeing an elder-law attorney first.
- Evidence: https://seniorsafetyadvice.com/alternatives-to-joint-bank-accounts-for-elderly-parents/
- Severity: 4

**P9. A plan chosen for the parent turns bad during a crisis.**
- Who: adult children who picked a parent's MA plan.
- What hurts: providers leave the network mid-year while the member stays locked in until fall. Off-formulary drugs fall outside the $2,100 out-of-pocket cap.
- How often: built into plan design. 41% of Medicare Rights helpline callers ask about costs and 25% about enrollment periods.
- Cost: out-of-pocket drug exposure and fewer care options.
- Workaround: 1-800-MEDICARE exception requests, SHIP counselors, forums.
- Evidence: "We really disliked having HMO and little choices when she was in ICU the past month." (https://www.agingcare.com/questions/mom-change-medicare-plan-during-open-enrollment-if-in-nursing-home-188703.htm) ; https://www.nerdwallet.com/insurance/medicare/learn/hospital-drops-medicare-advantage
- Severity: 4

**P10. After the death, recognition starts again at every institution.**
- Who: the executor, often the former POA agent.
- What hurts: the POA ends at death, and accounts freeze once the bank is notified. Each bank, card issuer, brokerage and utility has its own process and wants certified death certificates. Collectors sometimes chase the former agent.
- How often: every estate. No count of institutions per estate was found.
- Cost: cash is blocked just as funeral costs fall due, and probate takes months.
- Workaround: bank executor kits, early funeral release, estate services.
- Evidence: "After he died, the nursing home hounded me to pay the debt… they referred the matter to a collection agency" (https://www.consumerfinance.gov/consumer-tools/educator-tools/resources-for-older-adults/financial-security-as-you-age/when-a-loved-one-dies-and-debt-collectors-come-calling/) ; https://www.binehamgillen.com/blog/do-banks-freeze-accounts-when-someone-dies/
- Severity: 4

## Already tried

- **State Medicaid and plan portals:** broken logins and scripted support. No working proxy access route was found (P5).
- **CMS-1696 / CMS-10106:** formal proxy status, but the proxy carries the burden of proof indefinitely (P3).
- **Bank roles (POA, signer, joint owner):** bank-specific forms, and staff who default families to joint ownership (P3, P8).
- **SHIP counselors, the Medicare Rights helpline, Medicare Interactive:** free, but families must know to look. 3.6M+ queries in 2023 (P9).
- **Patient advocates and care managers:** they work, but cost $100–$300/hour and are unreimbursed (P2).
- **Daily money managers:** $25–$100/hour. No outcome data (P7).
- **EverSafe ($7.49–$24.99/month) and Carefull:** thin independent reviews. One Trustpilot user could not connect a bank and could only cancel by phone (https://www.trustpilot.com/review/eversafe.com). Carefull's own POA-acceptance guides suggest the bank problem is still unsolved (P3, P4).
- **Elayne, Empathy, Atticus:** the evidence is mostly Elayne's own content (P10).
- **CBPP Unwinding Watch:** exists because state data is unreliable. Aggregate only (P1).
