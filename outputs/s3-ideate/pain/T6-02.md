# T6-02, owner side: crawler load, block/allow/charge decisions, telling agents from bots

1. **Who:** Community-run docs platform admins (Read the Docs). **Site type:** FOSS docs hosting. **What hurts:** AI crawlers pulled large files hundreds of times/day from many IPs with no rate limiting, driving bandwidth and CPU load. **How often:** continuous, daily abuse pattern before mitigation. **Cost:** origin bandwidth ran ~800GB/day, estimated ~$50/day (~$1,500/month) if it had all hit origin. **Workaround:** temporarily blocked all Cloudflare-identified AI crawlers, added rate limiting, reconfigured CDN caching, cutting bandwidth ~75% (800GB/day to 200GB/day). **Evidence:** "AI crawlers need to be more respectful," 2024 (older than window, flagged), bandwidth drop and cost figures reported. Source: https://about.readthedocs.com/blog/2024/07/ai-crawlers-abuse/ (2024). **Severity: 5** — direct, quantified budget and infra hit on a volunteer-run public resource.

2. **Who:** GNOME sysadmin Bart Piotrowski. **Site type:** FOSS project GitLab infra. **What hurts:** overwhelming automated request volume; after deploying Anubis proof-of-work, only ~3.2% of requests passed the challenge, implying ~97% of traffic was bots. **How often:** ongoing/daily. **Cost:** not dollar-quantified, but described as "dramatically increasing bandwidth costs, service instability" for stretched volunteer maintainers. **Workaround:** Anubis proof-of-work gate. **Evidence:** "some open source projects now see as much as 97 percent of their traffic originating from AI companies' bots." Source: https://thelibre.news/foss-infrastructure-is-under-attack-by-ai-companies/ (2025). **Severity: 5** — near-total traffic capture, forces maintainer time into firefighting instead of project work.

3. **Who:** Drew DeVault, SourceHut founder/CEO. **Site type:** FOSS code-forge (git hosting). **What hurts:** "hyper-aggressive LLM crawlers at scale" hitting git logs and commit endpoints, rotating random IPs to look human, bypassing robots.txt. **How often:** "dozens of brief outages per week." **Cost:** DeVault reports spending "20-100% of my time in any given week mitigating" the crawlers — i.e., in bad weeks his entire work week. **Workaround:** deployed Nepenthes tarpit, which he admits "might degrade access to some web pages for users." **Evidence:** "Please stop externalizing your costs directly into my face," 17 Mar 2025. Source: https://drewdevault.com/blog/Stop-externalizing-your-costs-on-me/ (2025). **Severity: 5** — a solo/small-team operator losing most of a work-week to this, repeatedly, with real outages.

4. **Who:** Fedora sysadmin Kevin Fenzi. **Site type:** large FOSS distro infra, still volunteer/community-run ops. **What hurts:** scraper bots so aggressive that a whole country's traffic had to be blocked to keep the service up. **How often:** ongoing, prompted a drastic geographic block. **Cost:** not dollar-quantified; collateral cost is every legitimate Brazilian user and mirror losing access. **Workaround:** blocked all of Brazil at the network level. **Evidence:** "the AI scraper bots had gotten so aggressive, he had to block the entire country of Brazil from access." Source: https://tech.slashdot.org/story/25/03/26/016244/open-source-devs-say-ai-crawlers-dominate-traffic-forcing-blocks-on-entire-countries (2025). **Severity: 5** — extreme, blunt mitigation with heavy collateral damage to real users.

5. **Who:** Codeberg/Forgejo maintainer Earl Warren. **Site type:** FOSS code-forge (git hosting, nonprofit). **What hurts:** "Codeberg and the Forgejo infrastructure ... were both targeted by excessive crawling and/or DDoS." **How often:** sustained incident over weeks (thread runs Mar–May 2025). **Cost:** not dollar-quantified; described infra-wide targeting requiring multiple engineering interventions. **Workaround:** blocked issue filters and commit-by-commit crawling ("we only blocked issue filters and crawling repo by commits basically, and it solved the problem" — fnetX, 23 May 2025); tried a redirect experiment that failed because bots don't follow redirects. **Evidence:** quotes above. Source: https://codeberg.org/forgejo/discussions/issues/320 (2025). **Severity: 4** — sustained multi-week engineering effort on a donation-funded nonprofit forge.

6. **Who:** Earl Warren, on RSS-feed side effects of Anubis. **Site type:** FOSS code-forge. **What hurts:** blocking measures create collateral damage for legitimate tooling — "RSS feeds will bypass Anubis on code.forgejo.org. It needs a configuration change," implying feed readers were being blocked or mis-handled. **How often:** ongoing configuration churn as new edge cases surface. **Cost:** admin time re-tuning rules repeatedly. **Workaround:** manual Anubis config patches. **Evidence:** quote above, 24 May 2025. Source: https://codeberg.org/forgejo/discussions/issues/320 (2025). **Severity: 3** — smaller but recurring tax on maintainer attention.

7. **Who:** ProtonDB maintainer "bdefore." **Site type:** small community-run gaming compatibility database (free service). **What hurts:** a single crawler (Prerender) alone drove excess bandwidth bills and pushed the site toward Netlify's 1TB free-tier limit, despite an existing bulk data-dump offer bots ignored. **How often:** daily, ~30GB/day from crawling. **Cost:** ~$500/month in excess bandwidth charges attributable to that one crawler. **Workaround:** none effective yet; frustration that bots ignore the existing data-dump alternative. **Evidence:** "$500/month in excess bandwidth charges from Prerender crawler alone... 30GB of data per day." Source: Hacker News thread, https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45105230 (2026). **Severity: 4** — concrete monthly dollar cost on a free community tool with no ad revenue to offset it.

8. **Who:** Hosting-infra commenter "thaumaturgy." **Site type:** shared/managed hosting serving many small sites. **What hurts:** ~95% of traffic now bot-driven; page loads take a full 1s round trip mostly in database queries, and ~6 aggressive AI bots hitting at 1 request/second create daily "Slashdot effect"-style overload. **How often:** daily. **Cost:** not dollar-quantified, described as recurring outage-level load. **Workaround:** ongoing tuning, no full fix reported. **Evidence:** quote paraphrased above. Source: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45105230 (2026). **Severity: 4** — describes systemic load across many small hosted sites, not just one.

9. **Who:** Commenter "throw_m239339," describing a relative's mid-sized informational sites. **Site type:** independent informational/content sites. **What hurts:** bot traffic severe enough that the hosting provider demanded higher payment tiers. **How often:** ongoing, triggered a forced business decision. **Cost:** hosting price increase demanded by provider; response was to add login walls and consider hard paywalls just to survive traffic costs. **Workaround:** forced login walls, considering hard paywalls. **Evidence:** as summarized. Source: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45105230 (2026). **Severity: 4** — direct forced cost increase and access-model change for a small publisher.

10. **Who:** "RebeccaTheDev," admin of a niche railroading forum. **Site type:** hobbyist community forum. **What hurts:** traffic "quintupled" from crawler load, making the site unusable for actual human members. **How often:** sustained since crawler wave began; described as constant. **Cost:** admin describes it as "a neverending game of whack-a-mole" of blocking attempts, i.e., ongoing unpaid admin time. **Workaround:** repeated manual blocking rules, none fully effective. **Evidence:** "Neverending game of whack-a-mole" — quote as reported. Source: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45105230 (2026). **Severity: 4** — small volunteer-run community site with real usability breakdown for its actual users.

11. **Who:** Commenter "dizlexic," operator of a virtual/online art gallery site. **Site type:** small niche cultural/art site. **What hurts:** Claude's crawler hit "every route regardless of timeouts with no throttle," taking the site down. **How often:** taken down 3 times in 3 days. **Cost:** repeated full outages in a 72-hour window, no dollar figure given. **Workaround:** not specified as resolved at time of comment. **Evidence:** "taken down 3 times in 3 days by Claude crawlers pounding every route ... with no throttle." Source: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45105230 (2026). **Severity: 4** — acute, repeated full outages on a small site in a very short window.

12. **Who:** Commenter "hombre_fatal," operator of a roleplay/text forum. **Site type:** long-running niche hobbyist forum, "decades-old" codebase. **What hurts:** "Got 10x traffic overnight from AI bots," with cache-busting deep crawls hitting an old, unoptimized stack. **How often:** sudden onset, then sustained. **Cost:** not dollar-quantified. **Workaround:** not resolved at comment time. **Evidence:** quote above. Source: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45105230 (2026). **Severity: 3** — significant load spike on legacy infra, no dollar figure to anchor severity higher.

13. **Who:** Small/independent publishers broadly (industry-aggregated, not single first-person voice). **Site type:** independent content and news sites. **What hurts:** organic Google referral traffic to publisher sites fell 33-38% year-over-year, with smaller sites losing up to 60% of search referrals as AI Overviews answer queries on the results page. **How often:** ongoing trend through 2026. **Cost:** direct traffic and ad-revenue loss, unquantified in dollars per site. **Workaround:** none reliable; some sites pursue AI-citation optimization instead. **Evidence:** "smaller sites losing 60% of search referrals," zero-click rate 80-83% on AI Overview queries. Source: https://contently.com/2026/04/27/ai-overview-traffic-impact/ (2026) [industry-aggregated figures, not a named individual site]. **Severity: 4** — structural revenue erosion, though figure is aggregate rather than a single first-person account.

14. **Who:** Card-testing fraud victims on Shopify (small merchants, per Shopify Community forum thread titles e.g. "Card Testing Bot Attack Flooding Our Store"). **Site type:** small independent Shopify stores. **What hurts:** bots run stolen card numbers through checkout to find which cards still work; merchants wake up to a "pile of fraudulent orders that all need to be refunded," and payment-processing fees on those failed/refunded charges are not refunded. **How often:** described as recurring "bursts" of tiny same-product orders across many different cards. **Cost:** unrefunded processing fees per burst, plus downstream chargeback costs weeks later; advanced bot protection gated behind $2,000+/month Shopify plans, out of reach for small merchants. **Workaround:** third-party fraud apps (Signifyd, NoFraud, Fraud Filter) with chargeback-guarantee add-ons. **Evidence:** "the real cost arrives weeks later as chargebacks... Advanced bot protection is only available on $2000+/month plans." Source: https://community.shopify.com/t/card-testing-bots-are-using-shopify-stores-to-figure-out-which-stolen-cards-still-work/683204 (2025-2026). **Severity: 4** — direct out-of-pocket fee and chargeback cost, plus a protection tier priced above small-merchant budgets.

15. **Who:** Small Shopify merchants facing scalper bots. **Site type:** limited-drop/sneaker-style and flash-sale small stores. **What hurts:** scalper bots monitor inventory and complete checkout in "under two seconds," hoarding limited stock faster than any human customer, so real customers cannot buy. **How often:** every limited drop or flash sale. **Cost:** lost sales to real customers, reputational damage, unquantified in dollars. **Workaround:** Shopify's built-in fraud/bot filters plus manual monitoring. **Evidence:** "complete checkout in under two seconds — far faster than any human." Source: https://community.shopify.com/t/card-testing-bots-are-using-shopify-stores-to-figure-out-which-stolen-cards-still-work/683204 (2025-2026) [unverified: exact per-drop loss figures not given]. **Severity: 3** — clear pain pattern but no dollar anchor in this source.

16. **Who:** Small ecommerce/WordPress site owners using Wordfence. **Site type:** WordPress-based small shops and blogs. **What hurts:** Wordfence's rate-limiting and bad-bot heuristics throttle legitimate AI crawlers (GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot) the same as malicious scrapers because they "look almost identical" at the network layer, and there is no allowlist toggle for any AI crawler except Google. **How often:** any time a site owner wants selective AI access, i.e., a standing configuration gap. **Cost:** admin time manually managing firewall rules with no granular control. **Workaround:** manual firewall rule edits; no built-in fix. **Evidence:** "There is no 'AI bot allowlist' toggle in Wordfence yet... no equivalent dropdown for OpenAI, Anthropic, Perplexity, Meta, or Apple." Source: https://powerfulcombo.com/blog/wordfence-ai-bots/ (2026). **Severity: 3** — configuration/control gap rather than acute outage, but affects a very large base of small WordPress operators.

17. **Who:** Anubis users hitting JavaScript/RSS compatibility issues. **Site type:** FOSS sites and wikis running Anubis (e.g., FreeBSD wiki users per forum thread title). **What hurts:** Anubis requires modern JavaScript, blocking legitimate non-browser or privacy-hardened clients (e.g., JShelter plugin users) and, per a GitHub issue, blocking Thunderbird's RSS reader from fetching feeds. **How often:** standing side effect for any client that isn't a modern JS-capable browser. **Cost:** lost legitimate access, admin support burden explaining workarounds. **Workaround:** users told to disable privacy plugins like JShelter for the domain; devs say better bot-fingerprinting is a future fix. **Evidence:** GitHub issue "Anubis seems to block thunderbird RSS readers." Source: https://github.com/TecharoHQ/anubis/issues/721 (2025-2026). **Severity: 3** — real collateral damage to legitimate users/tools, though narrower than full outages.

18. **Who:** Site owners adjusting to Cloudflare's 15 Sept 2026 default AI-crawler block on ad-bearing pages. **Site type:** free-tier and new Cloudflare customers running ad-supported small sites. **What hurts:** the new default blocks "mixed-use" crawlers (search + AI training/agent use combined) from ad-bearing pages unless the owner opts back in, forcing every affected owner to review and adjust settings around a hard deadline. **How often:** one-time policy-driven review, but ongoing monitoring afterward as bots evolve. **Cost:** admin time to audit dashboard settings under deadline pressure; risk of losing legitimate search indexing if misconfigured. **Workaround:** manually adjusting Cloudflare bot-management settings; new "Pay Per Use" marketplace as an alternative to blocking outright. **Evidence:** "Cloudflare's default settings will block 'mixed-use' crawlers from any pages that host ads" as of 15 Sept 2026. Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/01/cloudflares-new-policy-pushes-ai-companies-to-pay-for-publishers-content/ (2026). **Severity: 3** — policy-driven admin burden and risk of self-inflicted traffic loss from misconfiguration, though it is also offered as relief for the blocking pain above.

## Incumbents

- **Cloudflare Bot Fight Mode / AI Crawl Control** — free/entry tools many small sites rely on first; owners report AI and legitimate-crawler traffic look alike at the network layer, causing both under- and over-blocking; new Sept 2026 defaults require active configuration to avoid losing wanted search traffic. https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/01/cloudflares-new-policy-pushes-ai-companies-to-pay-for-publishers-content/
- **Anubis (TecharoHQ)** — free proof-of-work gate widely adopted by GNOME, FFmpeg, WineHQ, Codeberg/Forgejo; effective at filtering non-browser bots (only ~3.2% of GNOME requests passed) but breaks RSS readers and JS-hostile privacy tools, requiring ongoing config patches. https://github.com/TecharoHQ/anubis/issues/721 ; https://thelibre.news/foss-infrastructure-is-under-attack-by-ai-companies/
- **Wordfence** — standard WordPress security plugin; no granular allowlist for individual AI crawlers beyond Google, so legitimate AI bots and malicious scrapers get the same rate-limit treatment. https://powerfulcombo.com/blog/wordfence-ai-bots/
- **robots.txt** — near-universally ignored by aggressive AI crawlers per multiple sources (SourceHut, Codeberg); crawlers spoof user-agents and rotate IPs to evade it. https://drewdevault.com/blog/Stop-externalizing-your-costs-on-me/
- **Shopify built-in fraud filters / third-party apps (Signifyd, NoFraud, Fraud Filter)** — help with chargeback guarantees but strongest bot protection is gated behind $2,000+/month plans, out of reach for small merchants who see the most card-testing abuse. https://community.shopify.com/t/card-testing-bots-are-using-shopify-stores-to-figure-out-which-stolen-cards-still-work/683204
- **Nepenthes and similar tarpits** — used by SourceHut as a last resort; the operator himself acknowledges the tradeoff degrades access "for some web pages for users." https://drewdevault.com/blog/Stop-externalizing-your-costs-on-me/
