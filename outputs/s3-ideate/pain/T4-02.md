# T4-02 pain: licensees and court filers

## Pain items

1. **Daily noon deadline for pawnbroker police reports** — Who: pawn shop owner/counter clerk. What hurts: must prepare and file a daily report of every item pawned/pledged/bought that day. Frequency: daily, by noon the following day (CA). Cost: up to $1,500 fine (1st offense), $5,000 (2nd), $25,000 (3rd+) plus possible license suspension/revocation. Workaround/tool: LeadsOnline. Evidence: "Every pawnbroker shall prepare a daily report... and shall file such report by noon of the following day with the chief of police" and penalty tiers up to $25,000 (https://www.simmrinlawgroup.com/california-business-and-professions-code-section-21628/); (https://www.egattorneys.com/california-business-and-professions-code-21628). Severity: 5 — criminal misdemeanor exposure plus license loss on a daily, unforgiving clock.

2. **Failure-to-report is a criminal, not just civil, violation** — Who: pawnbrokers. What hurts: knowing failure to comply with reporting duties is charged as a misdemeanor, not a fee. Frequency: per violation/transaction missed. Cost: up to 6 months jail on a 3rd offense plus the $25k fine. Workaround/tool: none named; dealers rely on manual double-entry into POS then LeadsOnline. Evidence: "a conviction under BPC 21628 allows the local police or licensing board to suspend or revoke your secondhand dealer license" (https://www.cronisraelsandstark.com/ca-bpc-21628). Severity: 5 — criminal record and livelihood risk from a clerical miss.

3. **LeadsOnline subscription cost stacked on top of reporting labor** — Who: pawn/secondhand dealers, often required by local police to subscribe. Cost: a law-enforcement forum poster cites LeadsOnline access costing "like $2000.00 a year" for departments, implying dealers separately bear per-transaction/reporting overhead. Frequency: annual/ongoing. Workaround/tool: LeadsOnline is itself the mandated tool, not optional. Evidence: forum discussion of LeadsOnline cost (https://forum.officer.com/forum/officers-and-law-enforcement-professionals-only/the-squad-room/127780-pawn-shops-leads-online-and-thieves). Severity: 3 — recurring cost layered on mandatory compliance labor. [unverified: exact dealer-side fee structure not confirmed]

4. **Scrap metal/secondhand dealers must display and comply with daily electronic reporting** — Who: scrap metal dealer counter staff. What hurts: must enter all required transaction information into electronic databases by close of business each day and post a sign disclosing reporting to police. Frequency: daily, close of business. Workaround/tool: state-run or municipal portals. Evidence: "Scrap metal dealers must enter all required information for the purchase or acquisition of motor vehicles and catalytic converters into electronic databases by the close of business each day" (https://dps.mn.gov/news/bca/new-rules-buying-scrap-metal-aim-help-curb-thefts). Severity: 4 — end-of-day crunch with theft-related liability if missed.

5. **Tow yard lienholder notice deadlines are short and vary by state** — Who: tow yard/impound lot clerk. What hurts: must send lienholder and owner notice within a narrow statutory window that differs by state (CA 15–30 days depending on value/reason; NV 15–21 days; FL 7 business days; VA 5 business days). Frequency: per every non-consensual tow. Cost/consequence: "if the tow company skips lienholder notification or sends it too late, the lien sale can be legally voided after the fact — meaning the tow company may owe the lienholder the full market value of the vehicle it sold improperly" (https://towingservicehub.com/blog/towing-lien-laws). Workaround/tool: state DMV portals (e.g., CT towing portal), certified mail. Severity: 5 — direct cash liability (full vehicle value) from a missed date, on rules that differ state to state.

6. **Both owner and lienholder notice must be right, or the whole sale is void** — Who: tow/storage lot staff. What hurts: "Missing either notification invalidates your entire lien sale process, even if you properly notified the owner." Frequency: per lien sale. Cost: loss of ability to recoup storage/tow fees; vehicle essentially stuck. Workaround/tool: manual DMV registration lookups plus certified mail tracking. Evidence: (https://towingservicehub.com/blog/towing-lien-laws). Severity: 5 — all-or-nothing compliance with no partial credit.

7. **Connecticut DMV complaint process outlasts the lien-sale clock** — Who: consumers complaining about CT tow operators, but symmetric pain for compliant tow yards operating under investigation uncertainty. What hurts: "the complaint process can take weeks, which is often longer than the period before a towing company is allowed to sell a car." Frequency: per disputed tow. Workaround/tool: CT DMV Consumer Complaint Center, CT towing portal. Evidence: (https://portal.ct.gov/dmv/commercial-and-industry-services/towing); summarized finding above. Severity: 3 — creates legal/operational limbo around sale timing.

8. **One Legal e-filing rejection rate around 10%** — Who: solo/small-firm paralegals and attorneys e-filing across counties. What hurts: roughly 1 in 10 e-filings bounce back needing rework. Frequency: per batch of filings, ongoing. Workaround/tool: One Legal's "Re-File" feature. Evidence: "approximately 10% of filings are rejected by the court" (https://www.onelegal.com/blog/one-legals-rejected-filing-re-submission-feature/). Severity: 4 — recurring rework tax on deadline-bound filings.

9. **Filer billed even when the court rejects the filing** — Who: solo filer using One Legal. What hurts: charged a service fee on a filing the court rejected, with no refund. Frequency: per rejected filing. Cost: fee paid for nothing, e.g. "$90 for each order" cited by a separate reviewer for duplicate orders they'd asked to consolidate. Workaround/tool: One Legal / InfoTrack e-filing service. Evidence: "charged a fee while my case was rejected by the court; One Legal is not willing to reimburse me" — Jean, Apr 30 2026 (https://www.trustpilot.com/review/onelegal.com). Severity: 4 — pays twice: once for the failed attempt, again for the fix.

10. **Filing service delays cost real court dates and possession timing** — Who: attorney/paralegal relying on e-filing service for a writ. What hurts: a service error delayed "execution of the writ and the acquisition of possession of the property by approximately one month" — Tom Lewis, Jan 27 2026 (https://www.trustpilot.com/review/onelegal.com). Frequency: per filing where the service errs. Severity: 5 — a month's delay on a time-sensitive legal remedy.

11. **Rework and duplicate data entry inside the e-filing tool itself** — Who: paralegal filing proofs of service. What hurts: "13 new proof of service docs and efiled all 13" then billed "for all the excessive work" after the vendor's own correction process multiplied the work; separately "unnecessary double data entry" requiring information submission twice. Frequency: per correction cycle. Evidence: Julia Wood, Dec 11 2025; George Garcia, Nov 19 2025 (https://www.trustpilot.com/review/onelegal.com). Severity: 3 — inflates hours and fees on what should be a single fix.

12. **Missed court hearing despite weeks of back-and-forth with the filing vendor** — Who: solo filer/paralegal. What hurts: "was not able to get [it] fixed in time for my court hearing today after I have been working with them to fix and review for weeks" — Julia Wood, Dec 11 2025 (https://www.trustpilot.com/review/onelegal.com). Frequency: per problematic filing. Consequence: missed hearing. Severity: 5 — the deadline is the hearing itself, not a portal setting.

13. **Court e-filing system outages force manual fallback with no deadline relief stated** — Who: any attorney/paralegal filing in the affected court. What hurts: "Washington State Court of Appeals is experiencing system unavailability with 'no estimated time for restoration'" and filers must switch to emailing every party individually; the notice gives no information on deadline extensions. Frequency: episodic but recurring across jurisdictions (multiple states' portals have had documented outages). Workaround/tool: email filing as stopgap, per division inbox. Evidence: WSBA notice, Nov 4 2024 (https://www.wsba.org/news-events/latest-news/news-detail/2024/11/04/court-of-appeals-e-filing-outage). Severity: 4 — deadline risk during an outage the filer cannot control, with no stated grace period.

14. **County-by-county e-filing rule variation for the same attorney** — Who: solo/small-firm attorneys and paralegals filing across multiple California counties. What hurts: each county court publishes its own e-filing technical requirements and mandate timelines (e.g., Alameda mandatory e-filing for most attorneys began a set date; family/probate/mental-health/adoption e-filing became mandatory on a separate later date in the same county). Frequency: ongoing, every new matter/county. Workaround/tool: reading each county's own PDF technical-requirements sheet. Evidence: Alameda County e-filing mandate pages (https://www.alameda.courts.ca.gov/news/mandatory-e-filing-attorneys-most-cases-begins-monday); (https://www.alameda.courts.ca.gov/system/files/e-filing-technical-requirements.pdf). Severity: 3 — no single national or even single-state rulebook to follow.

15. **Guardianship annual accounting is detailed and deadline-bound with no built-in flexibility** — Who: professional/court-appointed guardians and conservators, often assisted by paralegals. What hurts: must file a detailed annual accounting of all property received/disbursed and all property on hand, due on the anniversary date each year; courts recommend guardians log transactions weekly/monthly just to be ready. Frequency: annual, but requires continuous record-keeping to meet it. Cost: Florida courts charge an audit fee based on estate value on top of the guardian's own time. Workaround/tool: attorney or accountant retained to help complete the account (a paid fallback, itself a cost). Evidence: "Administrative Order 5.1 and Florida Statutes require the guardian of the property to file an Annual Accounting on or before the anniversary date of each year. Florida courts charge a fee for auditing annual accountings based on the value of the estate." (https://www.thompsonmcclarydefensefirm.com/how-to-file-an-annual-accounting-report-as-a-guardian-in-florida/). Severity: 4 — annual deadline with fee exposure and administrative burden that compounds for guardians managing multiple wards.

16. **Guardianship accounting errors trigger court audit follow-up, hearings, or added document requests** — Who: guardians/conservators, and by extension the paralegals who prepare filings for them. What hurts: "if discrepancies are found, the court may hold a hearing or request additional documents," adding cycles of rework after the original filing. Frequency: per flagged accounting. Workaround/tool: outside auditors or clerk's auditor review process. Evidence: (https://www.fljud13.org/portals/0/forms/pdfs/ejc/reviewsheetannual.pdf) and audit description (https://www.collierclerk.com/wp-content/uploads/2022.18-Annual-Guardianship-Report.pdf). Severity: 3 — turns a single annual filing into a multi-round process when errors are found.

17. **Pawn shop clerk wages sit well below national average despite compliance-heavy role** — Who: pawn shop clerks who handle both sales and mandatory daily police reporting. Cost: "The average American Pawn Clerk monthly pay in the United States is approximately $2,081, which is 29% below the national average" (Indeed data, 7 data points). Frequency: ongoing compensation gap. Evidence: (https://www.indeed.com/cmp/American-Pawn/salaries/Clerk?period=MONTHLY). Severity: 2 — proxy for low staffing investment relative to regulatory duty, single-employer sample. [unverified: single-company sample, not industry-wide]

18. **Tow companies risk owing full vehicle market value over a paperwork timing miss** — Who: tow/impound lot owner. What hurts: a lien sale conducted after a late or skipped notice can be voided, and the tow company can be on the hook for the full market value of a vehicle it already sold. Frequency: per contested lien sale. Cost: full vehicle value, uncapped by the (often much smaller) towing/storage fees actually owed. Workaround/tool: certified mail notice logs, DMV lookup portals. Evidence: (https://towingservicehub.com/blog/towing-lien-laws). Severity: 5 — the financial downside is disproportionate to the administrative task that caused it.

## Incumbents seen
- LeadsOnline: mandated reporting tool for pawn/secondhand dealers; cost noted by a law-enforcement forum poster as roughly $2,000/year on the department side, with dealer-side cost/time burden not itemized in available sources (https://forum.officer.com/forum/officers-and-law-enforcement-professionals-only/the-squad-room/127780-pawn-shops-leads-online-and-thieves).
- Towbook: reviews on Capterra and G2 are largely positive; the one recurring complaint is the program occasionally freezing and requiring a computer reset during busy workdays (https://www.capterra.com/p/94188/Towbook-Management-Software/reviews/).
- One Legal (An InfoTrack Company): ~10% filing rejection rate acknowledged by the vendor itself; user complaints include being billed for court-rejected filings, duplicate/consolidated-order overcharges, and rework requiring double data entry (https://www.onelegal.com/blog/one-legals-rejected-filing-re-submission-feature/, https://www.trustpilot.com/review/onelegal.com).
- InfoTrack: publishes its own "why was my filing rejected" support page, implying rejections are common enough to warrant a standing help doc (https://support.infotrack.com/efiling-basics/why-was-my-court-filing-rejected).
- State/county e-filing portals (e.g., Washington Court of Appeals): documented outage in Nov 2024 with no stated deadline relief, forcing email fallback (https://www.wsba.org/news-events/latest-news/news-detail/2024/11/04/court-of-appeals-e-filing-outage).
- Connecticut DMV Towing Portal System: used for lienholder/owner lookups; consumer complaint handling reported to take weeks, longer than the pre-sale window (https://portal.ct.gov/dmv/commercial-and-industry-services/towing).

## Numbers
- Approximately 13,500 pawn shops operate in the U.S. as of 2022; 48 of 50 states require a pawnbroker license (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Pawnbrokers_Association / https://nationalpawnbrokers.org/about/).
- CA pawnbroker failure-to-report penalties: up to $1,500 (1st offense), $5,000 (2nd), $25,000 (3rd+), plus possible license suspension/revocation (https://www.egattorneys.com/california-business-and-professions-code-21628; https://www.cronisraelsandstark.com/ca-bpc-21628).
- CA lien-sale timing: notice at least 31 but not more than 41 days prior to sale; registration info request within 15–30 days of tow depending on value/reason (https://www.dmv.ca.gov/portal/handbook/dismantlers-handbook-of-registration-procedures/lien-sales-and-abandoned-vehicles/lien-sale-procedure-for-vehicles-valued-or-more-or-stored-at-a-self-service-storage-facility-cc-3071/).
- One Legal: ~10% of e-filings rejected by courts (https://www.onelegal.com/blog/one-legals-rejected-filing-re-submission-feature/).
- American Pawn clerk average monthly pay ~$2,081, 29% below national average, based on 7 Indeed data points (https://www.indeed.com/cmp/American-Pawn/salaries/Clerk?period=MONTHLY).
