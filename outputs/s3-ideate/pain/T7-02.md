# T7-02: AI-generated vulnerability reports and insurance claim material (triage/verification pain)

### Security reports

1. **Who:** Daniel Stenberg, curl maintainer. **Setting:** curl project, HackerOne bug bounty. **What hurts:** Confirmed-vulnerability rate collapsed as AI slop reports flooded the queue, and each one still has to be read and disproven. **How often:** Sustained increase through 2025-2026; 20 submissions in the first 21 days of January 2026, 7 arriving in one 16-hour window. **Cost:** Confirmed rate fell from "north of 15%" pre-2025 to below 5% in 2025; zero real vulnerabilities from the January 2026 batch. **Current workaround:** Closed the paid HackerOne bug bounty entirely, effective Feb 1 2026; moved to unpaid GitHub Security Advisories/disclosure page. **Evidence:** "The never-ending slop submissions take a serious mental toll to manage and sometimes also a long time to debunk." / "Starting 2025, the confirmed-rate plummeted to below 5%. Not even one in twenty was real." Source: https://daniel.haxx.se/blog/2026/01/26/the-end-of-the-curl-bug-bounty/ (2026-01-26). **Severity: 5** — drove full closure of a paid program with an 87-vulnerability, $100k+ track record.

2. **Who:** Daniel Stenberg. **Setting:** curl, security.txt / disclosure policy. **What hurts:** Needing to publicly threaten and ban reporters to deter further unpaid triage load. **How often:** Ongoing as of policy update. **Cost:** Not quantified in dollars; described as mental-health risk to a "small number of active maintainers." **Current workaround:** security.txt now states no monetary compensation and warns bad reporters will be "banned and ridiculed publicly." **Evidence:** "We will ban you and ridicule you in public if you waste our time on crap reports." / "It is not in our power to change how all these people and their slop machines work. We need to make moves to ensure our survival and intact mental health." Source: https://socket.dev/blog/curl-shuts-down-bug-bounty-program-after-flood-of-ai-slop-reports (2026-01-23). **Severity: 5** — maintainer frames it as a survival/mental-health issue, not a minor annoyance.

3. **Who:** Elastic Security Labs / HackerOne program operators. **Setting:** Elastic's HackerOne bug bounty program. **What hurts:** Report volume outpacing human triage capacity. **How often:** H1 2026 alone. **Cost:** 1,390 reports received in H1 2026, more than full-year 2024 and 2025 combined; human analysts historically spent 30-60 minutes per report on "mechanical first-pass work"; roughly 70% of reports rejected during the analysis phase before reproduction is even attempted. **Current workaround:** Built an internal AI first-pass triage pipeline (analysis $0.50-$1.15/report, reproduction $0.80-$4.90/report on ~30% of submissions, ~$2 total per report) with human final call retained. **Evidence:** "1,390 reports in H1 2026 — more than the entire full-year totals for 2024 and 2025 combined." Source: https://www.elastic.co/security-labs/ai-vulnerability-triage-bug-bounty-hackerone (2026). **Severity: 4** — quantifies both the volume spike and the per-report cost of triage.

4. **Who:** Unnamed open-source maintainers (general, multiple projects). **Setting:** GitHub issues/security inboxes across projects. **What hurts:** Reports citing non-existent functions, fabricated commit hashes, and unreproducible vulnerabilities that still require investigation. **How often:** 20-40 security reports per week reported by some maintainers, nearly all AI-written, often with 3-5 duplicates each. **Cost:** Not separately dollarized; described as effectively "DDoS'ed." **Current workaround:** Pre-screening mechanisms, stricter submission filters, and shifting triage focus to "explaining to AI systems why reported behaviors fall outside a project's threat model." **Evidence:** High-profile maintainers describe being "effectively DDoS'ed" by low-effort, machine-written vulnerability claims. Source: https://theoutpost.ai/news-story/ai-bug-hunters-flood-open-source-security-with-reports-maintainers-struggle-to-separate-signal-from-noise-24455/ (2026). **Severity: 4** — recurring weekly load across many projects, not a single-project anomaly.

5. **Who:** Seth Larson, security developer-in-residence, Python Software Foundation; triager for CPython, pip, urllib3, Requests. **Setting:** Multiple Python-ecosystem projects' security inboxes. **What hurts:** "Low-quality, spammy, and LLM-hallucinated security reports" citing fake functions, fabricated commit hashes, unverified patches, and vulnerabilities that cannot be reproduced under any circumstances. **How often:** Described as an ongoing "uptick," not a single incident. **Cost:** Not dollarized; framed as wasted volunteer time. **Current workaround:** Public blog post urging reporters not to use AI systems for bug hunting; manual verification that cited functions/commits actually exist. **Evidence:** LLMs "don't actually understand code or human-level concepts like intent, common usage, and context," and reports "end up wasting volunteer time." Source: https://www.itbrew.com/stories/2025/01/06/devs-warn-ai-generated-inaccurate-bug-reports-are-slamming-open-source-projects (2025-01-06). **Severity: 4** — volunteer maintainer time is the scarcest resource in these projects and is directly named as the cost.

6. **Who:** Bug bounty platforms generally (HackerOne cited). **Setting:** Platform-wide submission review. **What hurts:** A majority of all submissions across the platform are invalid, meaning triage effort is spent mostly on noise. **How often:** Ongoing, worsening through 2025. **Cost:** HackerOne reports 60-80% of vulnerability submissions across its platform are invalid; Bugcrowd saw an increase of roughly 500 additional submissions per week in 2025. **Current workaround:** Platform-level triage teams and stricter scoping; some programs added AI-detection heuristics for submissions. **Evidence:** "HackerOne reports that 60-80% of vulnerability submissions across its platform are invalid, while Bugcrowd experienced an increase of 500 additional submissions per week in 2025." Source: https://www.linkedin.com/in/sethmlarson (via search aggregation citing GBHackers/Socket coverage, 2025-2026). **Severity: 3** — platform-wide, but figure is aggregated/secondhand [unverified exact primary source].

7. **Who:** Skilled triagers/maintainers generally. **Setting:** Any project accepting reports via HackerOne-style flow. **What hurts:** Per-report time cost of confirming an AI-hallucinated report is false, which also delays real reports behind it in the queue. **How often:** Per invalid report received. **Cost:** 30 minutes to several hours per report for a skilled triager depending on complexity. **Current workaround:** Manual reproduction of the proof-of-concept and code-path verification before closing. **Evidence:** "The amount of time it takes project maintainers to triage each AI-assisted vulnerability report... is tantamount to a DDoS attack on the project... anywhere from 30 minutes to several hours per report." Source: aggregated discussion referencing curl case, via search results tied to https://socket.dev/blog/curl-shuts-down-bug-bounty-program-after-flood-of-ai-slop-reports (2026). **Severity: 4** — direct time-cost figure with explicit queue-delay mechanism named.

8. **Who:** CVE/NVD program staff and downstream vulnerability database consumers. **Setting:** MITRE CVE program / NIST NVD. **What hurts:** Batches of technically bogus CVEs (e.g., SQLite) entering the record with CISA-supplied enrichment, on top of an already large backlog. **How often:** Backlog condition ongoing through 2025-2026. **Cost:** Backlog grew to more than 27,000 by end of 2025 per a Department of Commerce Inspector General report (May 2026); NVD moved to a triage model enriching only an estimated 15-20% of incoming CVEs, reclassifying roughly 29,000 backlog vulnerabilities as "Not Scheduled" (April 2026). **Current workaround:** Triage-only enrichment model rather than full review of every CVE. **Evidence:** "Six supposed SQLite vulnerabilities published in a larger batch by a new, obscure GitHub repository being complete garbage." Source: https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/08/03/ai-slop-pollutes-the-cve-pipeline-with-fake-vulns/5282462 (2026-08-03). **Severity: 4** — downstream institutional failure caused by unfiltered submission volume, with a specific backlog number.

9. **Who:** Bug bounty triagers (job-market role). **Setting:** Security triage analyst / bug bounty triage positions (HackerOne/Bugcrowd-adjacent employers). **What hurts:** Role explicitly built around validating incoming submissions and eliminating false positives at volume. **How often:** Full-time recurring duty. **Cost:** Pay range roughly $31.73-$66.83/hr for bug-bounty-program roles per aggregated job listings; duties named as "validating incoming submissions... eliminating false positives, validating reported vulnerabilities, assessing impact and severity." **Current workaround:** Dedicated triage staff/automation to pre-filter before human review. **Evidence:** Source: https://www.ziprecruiter.com/Jobs/Bug-Bounty-Program (2026, aggregated listings). **Severity: 2** — confirms the role and pay exist but gives no direct pain quote, only job-description language.

### Insurance claims

10. **Who:** Claims adjusters (general, U.S., multiple lines). **Setting:** Carrier-supplied AI tools for intake, damage assessment, medical-record summaries, payout processing. **What hurts:** Adjuster reviews of AI tools are overwhelmingly negative, with adjusters describing added rework rather than time saved. **How often:** Ongoing, reflected across many individual employer reviews. **Cost:** 98% of claims-adjuster Glassdoor reviews mentioning AI were negative — the highest negative share of any workforce group examined. **Current workaround:** Adjusters manually re-verify AI outputs against source documents before acting. **Evidence:** "AI is getting things wrong, and it's implementing more work onto the adjusters." Source: https://www.techspot.com/news/113688-claims-adjusters-ai-making-insurance-process-harder-not.html (2026). **Severity: 5** — near-universal negative sentiment from the people who must act on the AI output.

11. **Who:** Sandy Avina, former claims adjuster. **Setting:** General claims handling, document review. **What hurts:** AI systems hallucinate when reading imperfect source documents, producing summaries adjusters must catch before they cause wrong payouts. **How often:** Not given as a frequency, but described as a recurring risk in document intake. **Cost:** Not dollarized directly; risk framed as "inaccurate payout" when an AI summary omits a detail from a medical report. **Current workaround:** Manual re-checking of AI-generated summaries against the underlying medical report/document. **Evidence:** "A smudge on a document from an attorney" can cause a hallucination; "If an AI-generated summary leaves out an important detail from a medical report, it can also result in an inaccurate payout." Source: https://www.techspot.com/news/113688-claims-adjusters-ai-making-insurance-process-harder-not.html (2026). **Severity: 4** — names the specific failure mode (document quality triggering hallucination) and the downstream financial consequence.

12. **Who:** Claims adjuster (unnamed, quoted secondhand). **Setting:** Claims handling where adjuster relays carrier AI summary to claimant/attorney. **What hurts:** When the adjuster passes along an AI hallucination to a claimant or attorney, the adjuster personally absorbs the resulting anger, not the AI vendor or carrier. **How often:** Described as a recurring pattern ("when he inadvertently relayed those errors"). **Cost:** Not dollarized; cost is reputational/emotional labor borne individually by the adjuster. **Current workaround:** None described beyond after-the-fact damage control with the claimant. **Evidence:** The adjuster encountered "AI-driven hallucinations while reading claims summaries; when he inadvertently relayed those errors to claimants or their attorneys, he bore the brunt of their fury." Source: https://www.techspot.com/news/113688-claims-adjusters-ai-making-insurance-process-harder-not.html (2026). **Severity: 4** — shows the accountability gap: the human triager absorbs consequences for an AI-authored error.

13. **Who:** Claims adjusters (workforce-wide). **Setting:** Property/auto/workers' comp/bodily injury claims handling generally. **What hurts:** "AI fatigue" from being made to use and correct AI tools that add work rather than remove it. **How often:** Described as ongoing, workforce-wide exhaustion. **Cost:** Employment for claims adjusters dropped 21% between May 2025 and May 2026; entry-level postings fell 50% since 2025 even as adjusters report more AI-driven correction work, not less. **Current workaround:** None described; adjusters continue absorbing the extra verification load. **Evidence:** "There's an AI fatigue... We're pretty much exhausted as far as the amount of AI being shoved down our throats." — Geoffrey Conrad. Source: https://www.techspot.com/news/113688-claims-adjusters-ai-making-insurance-process-harder-not.html (2026). **Severity: 4** — combines workforce sentiment with a concrete employment-decline figure occurring alongside the AI rollout.

14. **Who:** Insurers' Special Investigations Units (SIU) and fraud teams. **Setting:** Auto and property claims with submitted photos/media. **What hurts:** Volume of AI-altered or fabricated claim media that must be forensically checked before payout. **How often:** Per claim, at meaningful scale. **Cost:** Shift Technology estimates 20-30% of insurance claims may contain AI-altered media; a March 2026 Verisk study found 98% of insurers agree AI-editing tools are fueling a rise in digital fraud and 99% say they've already encountered manipulated or AI-altered documentation. **Current workaround:** Forensic image analysis (metadata/pixel-level anomaly checks) by SIU teams. **Evidence:** Zurich's SIU "forensic analysis revealed metadata timestamps predating the claimed accidents by years, as well as pixel-level anomalies from AI editing." Source: https://www.simplesolve.com/blog/ai-altered-media-insurance-claims (2026) and https://eciks.org/6336-73684-insurance-industry-tackles-ai-generated-image-fraud-308-6b-annual-threat (2026). **Severity: 5** — very high prevalence estimate (20-30% of claims) combined with named forensic workload.

15. **Who:** Admiral (UK motor/home insurer) fraud teams. **Setting:** Auto and home claims. **What hurts:** Fraud volume tied to AI-generated evidence rose sharply, increasing investigation load. **How often:** Year-over-year, 2025. **Cost:** 71% year-over-year increase in fraud in 2025, driven in part by AI-generated evidence. **Current workaround:** Expanded fraud/SIU review processes (no tool named as sufficient). **Evidence:** "Admiral... reported a 71% year-over-year increase in fraud in 2025, driven in part by AI-generated evidence." Source: https://eciks.org/6336-73684-insurance-industry-tackles-ai-generated-image-fraud-308-6b-annual-threat (2026). **Severity: 4** — concrete year-over-year percentage from a named carrier.

16. **Who:** Fraud investigators / claims examiners handling documents generally. **Setting:** Multi-line claims involving invoices, medical records, and identity documents. **What hurts:** AI now lets claimants or organized rings produce fraudulent photos, invoices, medical records, and identities realistic enough to pass an initial review. **How often:** Roughly 1 in 50 forged documents is now estimated to be AI-generated; deepfakes account for 11% of first-party fraud; sophisticated multi-step fraud rose 180% year over year. **Cost:** Not separately dollarized per claim, but tied to a $308.6B industry-wide annual fraud threat estimate. **Current workaround:** Initial manual review followed by escalation to forensic/SIU checks when documents look suspicious. **Evidence:** "Deepfakes account for 11% of first-party fraud, sophisticated multi-step fraud rose 180% year over year, and roughly 1 in 50 forged documents is now AI-generated." Source: https://eciks.org/6336-73684-insurance-industry-tackles-ai-generated-image-fraud-308-6b-annual-threat (2026). **Severity: 4** — quantifies how often AI-fabricated material now clears first-pass review.

17. **Who:** Personal injury attorneys and adjusters reviewing AI-assisted demand letters. **Setting:** Bodily injury claims, demand-letter review. **What hurts:** Adjusters cross-reference ICD codes and dates in AI-drafted demand letters against medical records, and mismatches trigger flags/pushback that slow the claim. **How often:** Per demand letter where AI drafting is used; 37% of personal injury lawyers already use generative AI at work. **Cost:** Not dollarized per claim; described only as a flagging/dispute trigger. **Current workaround:** Adjusters manually cross-reference ICD codes and treatment dates against the underlying medical record before accepting a demand figure. **Evidence:** "Insurance adjusters cross-reference ICD codes against treatment records, and if the codes in the demand do not match the medical records, the adjuster flags the inconsistency." Source: https://www.inquery.ai/post/ai-demand-letter-tools-personal-injury-2026/ (2026). **Severity: 3** — names the specific verification action adjusters take but numbers are from a vendor-adjacent source [use with caution].

### Incumbents

- **HackerOne / Bugcrowd triage services** — platform-level human+automated triage exists, but HackerOne itself reports 60-80% of submissions platform-wide are invalid, and curl's own experience shows platform triage did not stop the flood reaching maintainers. Source: https://socket.dev/blog/curl-shuts-down-bug-bounty-program-after-flood-of-ai-slop-reports (2026-01-23).
- **GitHub Security Advisories / private vulnerability reporting** — curl's replacement channel after closing its paid bounty; addresses cost (no more payouts) but does not remove the need to read and reproduce each incoming report. Source: https://daniel.haxx.se/blog/2026/01/26/the-end-of-the-curl-bug-bounty/ (2026-01-26).
- **NVD triage model (NIST)** — moved to enriching only ~15-20% of incoming CVEs and reclassified ~29,000 backlog items as "Not Scheduled" rather than clearing the backlog; effectively a rationing response, not a fix. Source: https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-whitepaper-nvd-infrastructure-crisis-ai-vulnerability-di/ (2026).
- **Carrier AI claim summarizers (unnamed/general)** — deployed for intake, damage assessment and medical-record summarization, but adjusters report they "misclassify claims, generate hallucinated summaries, or misread uploaded documents, and then send the cleanup back to the humans." Source: https://www.techspot.com/news/113688-claims-adjusters-ai-making-insurance-process-harder-not.html (2026).
- **SIU forensic image-analysis (e.g., Zurich's internal SIU process)** — catches some AI-altered media via metadata and pixel-level anomaly checks, but only after claims reach investigation, and prevalence estimates (20-30% of claims with altered media) suggest much still gets through first-pass review. Source: https://www.simplesolve.com/blog/ai-altered-media-insurance-claims (2026).
