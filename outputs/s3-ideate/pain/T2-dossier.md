# T2 dossier: invoice intake and the e-invoicing switchover at small firms

Merged from `outputs/s3-ideate/pain/T2-01.md` (unstructured intake: fetch, extract, post, check) and `outputs/s3-ideate/pain/T2-02.md` (EU structured e-invoice mandates). Deadlines: Germany must receive from 1 Jan 2025 and issue from 2027/2028. Belgium receives and issues via Peppol from 1 Jan 2026, with fines from about April 2026. France receives from 1 Sept 2026, and small firms issue from 1 Sept 2027. Spain's Verifactu starts 1 Jan 2027 for companies and 1 July 2027 for autónomos. Spain's B2B date is disputed [unverified].

## Pain points

**P1. Manual keying is still the default.** Who: small-business bookkeepers and AP clerks. What hurts: invoices are re-typed into QuickBooks or Xero, with PO matching and expense coding done by hand. How often: every invoice, every day. Cost: about $15 per invoice manually. Across AP the average is $9.40 per invoice against $2.78 at best-in-class, with 9.2 days of cycle time against 3.1. More than 60% of invoices need a human touch. One person handles 32-40 invoices a day. AP data-entry pay averages $21.07/hr (Sept 2026). Workaround: dedicated clerks or outsourcing. Evidence: "70% of businesses still process invoices manually... $15 average cost," https://www.docuclipper.com/blog/invoice-data-entry/ ; Ardent Partners [vendor-sponsored], https://www.datocms-assets.com/80283/1744404602-ardent-partners-ap-metrics-that-matter-in-2025-pagero-final.pdf ; https://www.ziprecruiter.com/Jobs/Accounts-Payable-Data-Entry. **Severity 4.**

**P2. Capture tools miss their core promise, so people key by hand anyway.** Who: Hubdoc and Xero users. What hurts: auto-recognition fails, and bad scans need re-uploading. How often: per document, recurring. Cost: the full cost of manual re-entry on top of the subscription. Workaround: typing it in, or uploading the same file again. Evidence: "it hardly processes invoices automatically and they find themselves far too often having to manually type stuff in"; "load the same receipt into the app over 3 times"; TrustScore 2.1/5, https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/www.hubdoc.com. **Severity 4.**

**P3. Tax and VAT fields come out wrong or cannot be corrected.** Who: Hubdoc and Dext users with mixed-rate invoices. What hurts: invoices with more than one tax code break extraction, and VAT that is a penny off cannot be adjusted. Tax details published to Xero are "sometimes" wrong. How often: every mixed-tax invoice. Cost: manual correction after sync, and the risk of filing wrong VAT. Workaround: reviewing tax fields by hand after posting. Evidence: "doesn't work with any invoices that are slightly more complex ie. with different tax codes," https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/www.hubdoc.com ; "tax details sometimes are not captured correctly," https://www.capterra.com/p/160189/Dext/reviews/?page=5. **Severity 4.**

**P4. Duplicates get past the ledger's checks.** Who: QuickBooks Online and Hubdoc users. What hurts: QBO flags only exact vendor-plus-bill-number matches. Near-duplicates from formatting differences, variant vendor names, repeated imports, or "add" instead of "match" in the bank feed slip through, and Hubdoc creates duplicates on its own. How often: recurring, surfacing at month-end reconciliation. Cost: time spent running reports, undoing matches and re-matching. The dollar figure for duplicate payments was not found. Workaround: sorting the Unpaid Bills Detail report by hand. Evidence: the warning "can still miss near-duplicates caused by invoice-number formatting differences, inconsistent vendor names, repeated imports," https://quickbooks.intuit.com/learn-support/en-us/reports-and-accounting/is-there-an-alert-in-qbo-to-warn-of-duplicate-invoice-numbers/00/833418 ; "multiple duplications of Hubdoc creating additional transactions for absolutely no reason," https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/www.hubdoc.com. **Severity 3.**

**P5. Coding and line items need manual fixes or cost extra.** Who: Dext users, especially sole traders and small practices. What hurts: unknown suppliers get coded "unknown", and line-item extraction is an extra charge on top of escalating tiers. How often: every new vendor, and the subscription is ongoing. Cost: "time-consuming to adjust," and pricing that "escalates quickly." Workaround: fixing codes by hand, or skipping line items. Evidence: https://www.capterra.com/p/160189/Dext/reviews/?page=5 ; https://www.g2.com/products/dext/reviews [partly unverified]. **Severity 3.**

**P6. Sync between the AP tool and the ledger breaks.** Who: Bill.com users syncing to QuickBooks. What hurts: sync is intermittent, especially after QBD 2024 upgrades, and a bill edited on both sides causes conflicts. How often: "intermittent", and the vendor keeps a standing troubleshooting guide. Cost: time resolving conflicts by hand. Workaround: "check for sync conflicts regularly and clear them out." Evidence: https://quickbooks.intuit.com/learn-support/en-us/account-management/synch-with-bill-com/00/1464145. **Severity 3.**

**P7. Missing and late invoices hold up the close.** Who: small-business bookkeeping teams. What hurts: paperwork arrives after a purchase is approved, so someone has to chase it and keep a manual list of unresolved items. How often: every month-end. Cost: a delayed close. Not quantified. Workaround: a manual list, and paying first and filing the copy later. Evidence: "A missing invoice is common when a manager approves a purchase but sends the paperwork late," from aggregated close checklists (Chron.com, CPA South Florida) [URL not captured]. **Severity 3.**

**P8. Freight documents are re-keyed.** Who: billing staff at small freight brokers and carriers. What hurts: rate confirmations are built by hand from templates. Carrier invoices are audited against the BOL, the rate confirmation and the POD, then keyed into accounting. How often: every load. Cost: $19-32/hr roles, and the work scales with volume. Workaround: dedicated billing staff. Some staff drop the TMS feature for a manual method [unverified]. Evidence: "auditing and entering incoming carrier invoices into accounting systems," https://www.indeed.com/q-Freight-Billing-Specialist-jobs.html ; "open templates, copy details, fill in rates... format Rate Confirmations manually," https://arktms.com/blog/automated-document-generation-freight-broker-tms [vendor]. **Severity 3.**

**P9. Germany: firms are legally required to receive e-invoices but mostly can't.** Who: German firms, including Handwerk. What hurts: only 45% can receive e-invoices, and 96% still receive invoices by email. In craft trades only about half of roughly 1,200 inbound invoices a year are true e-invoices, so intake stays hybrid. How often: ongoing. Cost: about €3,000 one-time per firm, plus running costs, and "more effort instead of relief." Workaround: informal email and PDF intake. Evidence: Bitkom, 1,103 firms, 3 Dec 2024, https://www.bitkom.org/Presse/Presseinformation/Weniger-als-die-Haelfte-deutscher-Unternehmen-empfaengt-E-Rechnungen ; ZDH, 1,926 firms, Jan-Feb 2026, https://www.zdh.de/presse/veroeffentlichungen/pressemitteilungen/e-rechnung-fuer-betriebe-noch-mehr-aufwand-statt-entlastung/. **Severity 5.**

**P10. The legally required XML gets thrown away.** Who: German small firms and bookkeepers. What hurts: staff open the XRechnung, print it or save it as PDF, and delete the XML, even though the structured original must be kept for 8 years. How often: called "the most common mistake." Cost: audit exposure that firms don't know about. Workaround: free viewer tools. Evidence: "die XML-Datei verschwindet dann im E-Mail-Papierkorb," https://kostenlose-erechnung.de/ratgeber/e-rechnung-kleinunternehmer/. **Severity 4.**

**P11. Structured invoices can't be read by the people who have to approve them.** Who: Belgian SMEs and bookkeepers. What hurts: Peppol UBL is machine-only, the PDF is optional, and "many suppliers don't add one." How often: systemic since 1 Jan 2026. Cost: an extra viewer, and slower human review. Not quantified. Workaround: viewers, or asking suppliers for PDFs. Evidence: "Het bijvoegen van een PDF is optioneel in de Peppol-standaard," https://www.peppol-box.be/nl/blog/peppol-belgie-problemen-2026/. **Severity 4.**

**P12. Nobody can see whether e-invoices were delivered or whether the firm is even registered.** Who: Belgian SMEs. What hurts: owners assume they are "on Peppol" without confirming the registration is active, and can't tell whether invoices they sent arrived. Fines started around April 2026 (amounts unverified). How often: ongoing. Cost: silent delivery failures, late payment and fines. Workaround: the accountant checks by hand. Evidence: "Veel kmo's gaan ervan uit dat ze 'wel op Peppol zitten'," https://www.peppol-box.be/nl/blog/peppol-belgie-problemen-2026/. **Severity 4.**

**P13. Issued e-invoices fail validation or get rejected.** Who: small firms issuing in DE and FR. What hurts: software-generated XRechnung fails the official validator (for example, missing bank data), with no fix date from the vendor. French platforms automatically reject invoices with bad formats, missing mandatory fields or SIREN/SIRET mismatches, and the payment cycle stops until someone corrects them. How often: a risk on every invoice, by design. Cost: late payment and rework on each rejection. Workaround: reading the rejection codes and fixing by hand. Evidence: "vom Validator des Serviceportals Baden-Württemberg beanstandet," https://help.zoho.com/portal/hi/community/topic/books-generiert-keine-valide-xrechnung ; "Le refus... bloque le cycle de paiement," https://www.compta-online.com/facture-electronique-codes-rejet-plateforme-agreee-ao8532. **Severity 4.**

**P14. France: firms must pick and connect a platform or stop invoicing.** Who: TPE/PME and micro-entrepreneurs. What hurts: 150 registered plateformes agréées (as of 3 Sept 2026) and no default choice. Without a connected platform a firm "ne pourrez plus émettre ni recevoir vos factures." How often: a one-time choice with a hard legal cliff (receiving since Sept 2026, issuing from Sept 2027). Cost: invoicing stops if nothing is connected, plus lock-in risk. Workaround: comparison guides and the accountant's recommendation. Evidence: https://www.pennylane.com/fr/fiches-pratiques/facture-electronique/liste-des-pdp ; https://facturation-electronique-tpe.fr/reforme/plateforme-agreee-pdp/. **Severity 5.**

**P15. Advisers absorb the switchover, and clients pay for it.** Who: Steuerberater, Belgian accountants, Spanish gestores. What hurts: advisers get clients ready one by one. Belgian accountants charge clients for booking PDFs by hand. Spanish advisers clean up after AEAT messages that set off "alarma" about Verifactu, and they report that e-invoicing, real-time reporting and certified software are "three different obligations with different schedules." The Verifactu date has already moved once. How often: every client and every rule change. Cost: in Germany this is billed as time-based "Sonderarbeiten". Belgian costs are passed on to clients. Neither is quantified. Workaround: explainer guides, public statements from professional bodies, and new fee categories. Evidence: "Handmatig inboeken van PDF's kost hen geld — en dat rekenen ze door," https://www.peppol-box.be/nl/blog/peppol-belgie-problemen-2026/ ; https://www.haufe.de/steuern/steuerwissen-tipps/umfrage-hoher-aufwand-bei-e-rechnung-fuer-betriebe_170_689638.html ; https://www.consejogestores.org/noticias/los-gestores-administrativos-desmontan-la-alarma-sobre-verifactu-y-factura-electronica/ ; https://www.asesoratepymes.com/verifactu-vs-factura-electronica/. **Severity 3.**

## Already tried

- **Dext:** tax fields sometimes wrong on sync, suppliers coded "unknown", line items cost extra, and pricing is opaque and escalating (P3, P5).
- **Hubdoc (Xero):** 2.1/5 on Trustpilot. Recognition fails, it creates duplicates, it can't handle mixed tax codes, and files need re-uploading (P2-P4).
- **Bill.com:** intermittent QuickBooks sync and conflicts from editing on both sides (P6).
- **QuickBooks Online native checks:** duplicate detection only catches exact matches, and add-versus-match mistakes in the bank feed create duplicates (P4).
- **Freight TMS rate-con features:** some users find them slower than their manual method [unverified]. Re-keying at small brokers continues (P8).
- **General capture market:** tools claim about 99% accuracy (per `gates/gate-B.md`), yet first-person reviews describe manual fallback. First-person complaints about AutoEntry, Nanonets, Rossum, Docparser and Veryfi were not surfaced.
- **DATEV:** the dominant tool for advisers, but e-invoicing is "nicht im Fokus" for many Mittelstand clients, so the gap is client adoption. https://handwerk.com/steuern-und-finanzen/e-rechnung-bei-vielen-mittelstaendlern-nicht-im-fokus [vendor-adjacent]
- **sevDesk:** mixed Trustpilot reviews, with booking and duplication complaints during the compliance window. https://www.trustpilot.com/review/www.sevdesk.de
- **Zoho Books:** its XRechnung output fails the official validator (P13).
- **French plateformes agréées (Pennylane, Dougs, Abby, Kolecto, EBP, Docaposte, B2Brouter and others):** about 150 options. The comparison guides are themselves evidence of friction in choosing (P14).
- **Belgian Peppol access points (e-invoice.be, D Soft, Adminbuddy, Peppol OK, Combell):** pricing ranges from free to several hundred euros a month, with per-invoice fees from about €0.25 and setup from €0-500. There is no standard, and none of them fixes the delivery-visibility problem (P12). https://e-invoice.be/blog/kosten-peppol-e-facturatie
- **Spanish "Verifactu-compatible" software:** sold ahead of the mandate, which added to the alarm (P15).
