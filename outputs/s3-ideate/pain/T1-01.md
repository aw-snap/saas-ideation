# T1-01, pre-service: eligibility and prior authorization

1. **Who:** Physicians and practice staff generally (AMA survey base, 60% specialists/40% PCPs). **Portal:** payer portals generally. **What hurts:** volume and time spent completing PA requests. **How often:** weekly, ongoing. **Cost:** 39 PA requests per physician per week; 13 hours of physician-and-staff time weekly. **Workaround:** none named, absorbed as overhead. **Evidence:** "practices complete 39 prior authorization requests per physician, per week" and average 13 hours/week completing them, 2024 AMA survey of 1,000 physicians (Dec 2024). Source: https://www.ama-assn.org/practice-management/prior-authorization/fixing-prior-auth-nearly-40-prior-authorizations-week-way — **Severity 5**: high-frequency, large recurring time cost documented at scale.

2. **Who:** Practices with dedicated PA staff (40% of physicians per AMA). **What hurts:** need for full-time staff whose sole job is chasing authorizations. **How often:** continuous, full-time roles. **Cost:** staffing cost of dedicated FTEs. **Workaround:** dedicating multiple employees. **Evidence:** gastroenterologist quoted: "We have four full-time employees whose sole focus is on obtaining prior authorization for medications." Source: https://www.ama-assn.org/practice-management/prior-authorization/fixing-prior-auth-nearly-40-prior-authorizations-week-way (2024) — **Severity 4**: recurring headcount cost, but limited to practices with heavy PA volume (e.g., specialty medication).

3. **Who:** Physicians/staff (MGMA member practices). **What hurts:** rising volume of PA requirements forces hiring/reassignment just to keep up. **How often:** ongoing, worsening year over year per MGMA. **Cost:** added headcount that "could otherwise go toward patient care." **Workaround:** hiring or reassigning existing staff to PA work. **Evidence:** "92% of surveyed medical group practices reported hiring or reassigning staff solely to handle the growing volume of PA requests" (MGMA regulatory burden reporting, cited via search 2024-2025). Source: https://www.mgma.com/articles/the-prior-authorization-landscape-in-2025 — **Severity 4**: widespread (92%) structural staffing burden.

4. **Who:** Practice staff processing a single PA. **What hurts:** multiple staff touch one request, and requests take longer than casual estimate. **How often:** per PA request. **Cost:** "60% of practices indicated that at least three employees are typically involved in completing a single PA request, while 35% reported spending upwards of 35 minutes on average per request." **Workaround:** none, manual hand-offs between staff. **Evidence:** as above, MGMA (2024-2025). Source: https://www.mgma.com/articles/the-prior-authorization-landscape-in-2025 — **Severity 4**: shows the work is not a single-person task, multiplying labor cost per request.

5. **Who:** Physicians/practices (86% of MGMA respondents). **What hurts:** payer PA demands keep increasing year over year, not stabilizing. **How often:** annual trend. **Cost:** rising administrative cost, "needlessly driving up administrative costs for medical groups" (MGMA CEO quote). **Workaround:** none. **Evidence:** "86% of respondents reported prior authorization requests increased in the past year." Source: https://www.mgma.com/mgma-stats/payer-prior-authorization-requirements-on-physicians-continue-rapid-escalation-increasing-practice-overhead-and-delaying-patient-care (2017, older; direction reaffirmed by 2024-2025 MGMA articles above) [older, mark year] — **Severity 3**: trend evidence rather than current-year hard number, but corroborated by newer sources.

6. **Who:** Physician (dermatologist practice, per AMA reporting). **What hurts:** total time burden per week exceeds even the survey average. **How often:** weekly. **Cost:** 15 hours/week reported. **Workaround:** absorbed by office staff. **Evidence:** "15 hours per week reported by one dermatologist and office staff." Source: https://www.ama-assn.org/practice-management/prior-authorization/fixing-prior-auth-nearly-40-prior-authorizations-week-way (2024) — **Severity 3**: single first-person data point, but concrete and above-average.

7. **Who:** Physicians (2025 AMA survey, n=1,000). **What hurts:** PA-caused delay in care is near-universal. **How often:** "at least some of the time" reported by the large majority. **Cost:** clinical/quality cost, not dollar figure. **Workaround:** none. **Evidence:** "93% of physicians reported that prior authorization can cause care delays at least some of the time." Source: https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/healthcare-information-technology/providers-frustrated-with-prior-authorization-workflows-5-stats-to-know/ (May 2025, citing 2025 AMA Prior Authorization Physician Survey) — **Severity 5**: near-universal, direct patient-care impact.

8. **Who:** Physicians (2025 AMA survey). **What hurts:** patients give up on treatment because of PA delay/friction. **How often:** "at least sometimes" for 82% of physicians; zero physicians said it never happens. **Cost:** lost/delayed care, potential downstream cost to practice from abandoned treatment. **Workaround:** none. **Evidence:** "82% reported that prior authorization can at least sometimes lead to patients abandoning treatment plans." Source: https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/healthcare-information-technology/providers-frustrated-with-prior-authorization-workflows-5-stats-to-know/ (May 2025) — **Severity 5**: patient-harm and lost-revenue outcome, broad base.

9. **Who:** Physicians (2025 AMA survey). **What hurts:** serious adverse patient events tied to PA wait. **How often:** reported by more than a quarter of physicians as having occurred. **Cost:** clinical harm; hospitalization in 19-24% of cases depending on source cut. **Workaround:** none — escalation/peer-to-peer is the only lever and it is slow. **Evidence:** "24% of physicians say prior authorization wait led to hospitalization of patient." Source: https://www.beckersasc.com/asc-coding-billing-and-collections/24-of-physicians-say-prior-authorization-wait-led-to-hospitalization-of-patient/ (2025) — **Severity 5**: worst-case outcome, directly tied to PA wait time.

10. **Who:** PA/insurance verification/authorization specialists (job postings, national). **What hurts:** the job itself is defined by chasing authorizations, tracking denials and re-submitting, at modest pay. **How often:** daily, full-time role. **Cost:** average pay ~$21.85/hour; annual average ~$43,459-$55,431 depending on source. **Workaround:** hiring dedicated specialist roles (see item 2). **Evidence:** "The average salary for a prior authorization specialist is $21.85 per hour in the United States," and duties include "document and track all communication attempts with insurance providers and health plans, follow up on all denials." Source: https://www.ziprecruiter.com/Salaries/Prior-Authorization-Specialist-Salary and https://cookcountyhealth.org/wp-content/uploads/9983-PriorAuthorizationSpecialist-Finance-RevenueCycle-1.12.2024-public.pdf (2024-2026) — **Severity 3**: confirms a paid role exists purely to do this manual work, but is indirect (job-ad language, not a first-person complaint).

11. **Who:** Physician office staff, phone-based PA (pre-portal-era but still common per forum poster, physician, 20+ year SDN member). **What hurts:** staff time on unpaid administrative work for procedures the practice is not reimbursed for handling. **How often:** per MRI/imaging order. **Cost:** "20-30 minutes on the phone to get an MRI authorized for which we will be paid nothing," roughly "$15-20 per MRI for prior auth" in staff time. **Workaround:** considering outsourcing, or billing the patient for the time. **Evidence:** "It's entirely another when I have a staff member spending 20-30 minutes on the phone to get an MRI authorized for which we will be paid nothing." Source: https://forums.studentdoctor.net/threads/prior-authorization-woes.1281166/ (thread from 2017, marked older; nature of complaint recurs in newer surveys above) — **Severity 4**: concrete unpaid-labor framing, first-person, though older.

12. **Who:** Practices using CoverMyMeds as a PA vendor/portal add-on. **Portal:** CoverMyMeds. **What hurts:** the tool covers submission but not the full lifecycle; status can be misreported. **How often:** recurring, per submission. **Cost:** not stated in dollars; rework time implied. **Workaround:** staff must separately track post-submission status and handle denials/appeals manually. **Evidence:** "CoverMyMeds speeds the initial electronic submission, [but] does not cover the full prior authorization lifecycle, meaning delays can still occur in post-submission processes," and "the platform occasionally returns incorrect information (such as telling users an authorization wasn't submitted when it actually went through)." Source: aggregated from Capterra/G2 review summaries, https://www.capterra.com/p/154792/CoverMyMeds-Platform/reviews/ and G2 listings (2026) [unverified — specific reviewer quote not directly retrieved, summarized from search aggregation] — **Severity 3**: incumbent-tool-specific gap, but sourcing is a secondary summary, not a pulled verbatim review.

13. **Who:** Practices processing PA manually (phone/fax/email) vs. via payer portal. **What hurts:** manual channels take much longer than portal, and portal itself is still manual relative to full electronic (X12 278) automation. **How often:** per transaction, industrywide. **Cost:** 24 minutes per PA via phone/fax/email vs. 16 minutes via portal vs. automated; per-transaction cost $3.41-$10.97 manual vs. $0.05-$5.79 electronic. **Workaround:** using payer portals instead of phone/fax where available (still manual, not fully automated). **Evidence:** "providers and staff spend 24 minutes per prior authorization request when using phone, fax, or email, while for those working through health plan portals, each prior authorization takes 16 minutes per transaction," and "only 35% of medical industry prior authorizations are conducted fully electronically using the X12 278 transaction." Source: 2024 CAQH Index, https://www.ajmc.com/view/2024-caqh-index-foresees-major-opportunity-for-health-care-savings and https://www.caqh.org/blog/new-caqh-index-reveals-20b-savings-opportunity-to-cut-waste-reduce-costs-and-improve-patient-access (2024) — **Severity 4**: industry-scale benchmark showing portal work is still slow and mostly manual.

14. **Who:** Practices subject to Medicare Advantage PA rules. **What hurts:** volume of PA requests and denial rate even when clinically warranted. **How often:** annual aggregate, 2023 data. **Cost:** of 50+ million PA requests submitted to MA insurers, 3.2 million were partially or fully denied; 81.7% of appealed denials were overturned, implying most initial denials were avoidable friction. **Workaround:** appeal (out of this half's scope, but the initial denial and appeal-eligibility determination touches the portal at submission). **Evidence:** "50+ million prior authorization requests submitted to Medicare Advantage insurers" and "81.7% of appealed denials were fully or partially overturned." Source: https://www.ama-assn.org/practice-management/prior-authorization/fixing-prior-auth-nearly-40-prior-authorizations-week-way (2024, citing KFF/CMS MA data) — **Severity 4**: shows scale of wasted initial-submission effort given high overturn rate.

15. **Who:** Practices deciding whether to keep PA work in-house or outsource, including offshore options. **What hurts:** in-house staff cost vs. outsourced/offshore staff cost gap is large enough that vendors market against it directly. **How often:** ongoing staffing decision. **Cost:** offshore eligibility-verification staff quoted at "$399/week (volume discounts to $299/week)... compared to $18-22/hour for US-based staff," a claimed 65-70% savings. **Workaround:** outsourcing/offshoring eligibility and PA work. **Evidence:** as above, https://staffingly.com/insights/blog/eligibility-and-benefits-verification-process-bpo-outsourcing-india-philippines/ (2026) [vendor marketing source, treat cost figures as vendor-asserted, not independently verified] — **Severity 3**: reveals cost pressure practices face, but source is a vendor's own marketing copy.

16. **Who:** Physicians broadly (2025 AMA survey). **What hurts:** clinical outcomes suffer from the PA process, not just administrative time. **How often:** widespread. **Cost:** 94% report somewhat/significantly negative outcome impact; 29% report a serious adverse event. **Workaround:** none. **Evidence:** "94% said that prior authorization has a somewhat or significantly negative impact on patient clinical outcomes, with more than 1 in 4 (29%) reporting that it caused a serious adverse event for a patient in their care." Source: search aggregation of 2025 AMA Prior Authorization Physician Survey, https://www.ama-assn.org/practice-management/prior-authorization/exhausted-prior-auth-many-patients-abandon-care-ama-survey (May 2025) — **Severity 5**: outcome-level harm at scale.

17. **Who:** Practices, post-CMS-0057-F (2027). **What hurts:** even after the FHIR API mandate, large parts of PA work stay manual because the rule does not cover all payer types. **How often:** ongoing after Jan 2027 go-live. **Cost:** not quantified; structural gap. **Workaround:** none yet; practices will still do manual PA for excluded plans. **Evidence:** "CMS-0057-F requires impacted payers — Medicare Advantage organizations, state Medicaid and CHIP fee-for-service programs, Medicaid managed care plans, CHIP managed care entities, and Qualified Health Plan issuers on the Federally-facilitated Exchanges — to implement... four FHIR-based APIs," while "a commercial health plan is not automatically subject to CMS-0057-F simply because it offers employer-sponsored coverage" and traditional Medicare/standalone Part D are excluded. Source: search aggregation of CMS rule commentary, https://www.cms.gov/initiatives/burden-reduction/overview/interoperability/policies-regulations/cms-interoperability-prior-authorization-final-rule-cms-0057-f (rule fact sheet, current as of 2026) — **Severity 4**: defines a durable, named gap: commercial and traditional Medicare PA work stays manual past the 2027 deadline.

## Incumbents

- **Availity / NaviNet** — multi-payer portals used for eligibility and PA; search aggregation notes needing multiple identifying fields (DOB, NPI) to look up plans correctly, a friction point in daily use. Source: https://www.g2.com/products/essentials/reviews?page=2 (undated, aggregated).
- **CoverMyMeds** — speeds initial PA submission but does not cover the full lifecycle (status tracking, denials, appeals, peer-to-peer scheduling) and has been reported to show incorrect submission status. G2 rating 3.7/16 reviews. Source: https://www.g2.com/products/glidian and Capterra listing, https://www.capterra.com/p/154792/CoverMyMeds-Platform/reviews/ (2026).
- **CAQH-tracked eligibility/PA channels (phone/fax/email vs. portal vs. full X12 278 automation)** — portal-based PA still takes 16 minutes per transaction versus a fully-electronic 5-minute-class transaction implied by the $0.05 automated cost figure; only 35% of industry PA volume is fully electronic. Source: https://www.caqh.org/blog/new-caqh-index-reveals-20b-savings-opportunity-to-cut-waste-reduce-costs-and-improve-patient-access (2024).
- **Offshore/outsourced eligibility and PA staffing vendors (e.g., Staffingly)** — marketed as a lower-cost substitute for in-house verification/PA staff, implying the in-house manual-portal workflow is expensive enough to outsource. Source: https://staffingly.com/insights/blog/eligibility-and-benefits-verification-process-bpo-outsourcing-india-philippines/ (2026).
