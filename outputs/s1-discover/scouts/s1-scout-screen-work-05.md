# Scout slice: screen-work-05 — software used/bought by AI agents and their supervisors

## Findings

1. On the WebArena-style production gap, one analysis reports "A 78 percent WebArena score and a 22 percent production success rate is the typical gap," attributing it to real-world conditions benchmarks don't model: DOM drift, modals, login state, rate limits, irreversible actions. Dated June 2026. Affects: agent builders. Source: https://futureagi.com/blog/evaluating-browser-use-agents-2026/ (2026-06)

2. A subgoal-driven trajectory analysis found "mid-task stuck behavior in nearly 50% of WebArena-Lite trajectories for Gemini-2.5-Pro," meaning agents freeze mid-task even on a benchmark, not just in production. Dated 2026. Affects: agent builders/evaluators. Computer-centric: yes. Source: https://futureagi.com/blog/evaluating-browser-use-agents-2026/ (2026)

3. On CAPTCHA specifically, general AI agents face roughly 60% failure rates on modern CAPTCHA systems, and even LLM-enhanced specialized solvers only reach "60+% success rates in mixed CAPTCHA scenarios" [unverified aggregation, exact source page not opened]. Affects: builders of browser/computer-use agents. Computer-centric: yes. Source: search aggregation citing 2026 benchmark work (arXiv/Open CaptchaWorld family) — https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.24878

4. On the Open CaptchaWorld benchmark, "the strongest performer, OpenAI-o3, reaches 40.0%, followed by GPT-4.1 and Gemini2.5-Pro at 25.0%, while other models including Claude and GPT-4o variants perform between 5.0% and 20.0%," showing most frontier models fail most CAPTCHA challenges. Paper dated May 2025. Affects: agent builders. Computer-centric: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.24878 (2025-05)

5. A GitHub Discussion thread titled "Browseruse Ai agents are stuck in CAPTCHAS?" opened by user heyibad states: "When ever it visits google.com or other website having captchas," the agent gets stuck; follow-up posts through July 3, 2025 continued reporting persistent blocking with no definitive fix, including questions about paying third-party solvers like 2Captcha. Affects: open-source agent-framework developers. Computer-centric: yes. Source: https://github.com/browser-use/browser-use/discussions/1695 (2025-05 to 2025-07)

6. Cloudflare Radar data cited by CEO Matthew Prince (June 2026) showed "automated requests had crossed 57% of all HTML web traffic globally, surpassing human traffic for the first time," the infrastructure backdrop driving sites to harden bot defenses against agents. Affects: site operators and, downstream, agent builders who get blocked. Computer-centric: yes. Source: https://techscoop.substack.com/p/captchas-and-bot-detection-are-failing (2026-06)

7. Starting September 15, 2026, Cloudflare's default policy shifts to "block training and agent use for pages with ads" for new customers/sites, blocking "mixed-use" crawlers that blend search, agent use and training unless the site owner opts in otherwise. Affects: agent builders who scrape/browse third-party sites as part of workflows. Computer-centric: yes. Source: https://fastcrw.com/blog/cloudflare-ai-crawler-block-september-2026 (2026-07)

8. Cloudflare launched "Pay Per Crawl" in July 2025 as a marketplace for publishers to charge AI companies per crawl, then on July 1, 2026 extended it toward "pay per use" — charging "when the content actually creates value inside an AI product, not merely when it is retrieved" — with Ceramic.ai and You.com as first partners. Affects: agent/AI builders who now must pay for content access previously free. Computer-centric: yes. Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/01/cloudflares-new-policy-pushes-ai-companies-to-pay-for-publishers-content/ (2026-07-01)

9. Amazon sued Perplexity AI over its Comet browser's shopping agent, "accusing Perplexity AI of covertly accessing customer accounts and disguising AI activity as human browsing"; a US federal judge granted a preliminary injunction finding Perplexity "likely violated federal and California computer fraud laws," ordering it to pause Comet's ability to shop on Amazon. Affects: computer-use/shopping agent builders and the vendors whose sites resist them. Computer-centric: yes. Source: https://ppc.land/court-blocks-perplexitys-comet-browser-from-amazons-accounts/ (2025/2026, exact date on page)

10. Reddit filed a federal lawsuit on October 22, 2025 against Perplexity and three data-scraping companies "for allegedly bypassing technological controls to access and scrape Reddit content," and separately sued Anthropic over training-data use without consent. Affects: agent/crawler builders relying on Reddit content. Computer-centric: yes. Source: https://techcrunch.com/2024/06/25/reddits-upcoming-changes-attempt-to-safeguard-the-platform-against-ai-crawlers (lawsuit reported 2025-10-22)

11. llms.txt adoption remains modest: "A SE Ranking study of 300,000 domains found a 10.13% adoption rate after eighteen months," and crawler interest from search/answer bots is "negligible, with only 408 llms.txt requests targeted directly out of over 500M AI bot visits across a 90-day window," while IDE coding agents (Cursor, Windsurf, Claude Code, Copilot, Cline, Aider) "routinely fetch /llms.txt and /llms-full.txt when pointed at documentation sites." Affects: developers relying on agent-readable docs; documentation-site operators. Computer-centric: yes. Source: https://codersera.com/blog/llms-txt-complete-guide-2026/ (2026-05)

12. Agent payment infrastructure is scaling but immature: "The Coinbase-led x402 protocol processed roughly 165 million agent transactions and $50 million in cumulative volume across 69,000 active agents by April 2026," using an HTTP 402 response plus stablecoin signature to complete machine-to-machine payment. Affects: agents that need to pay for API/content access; vendors building agent-facing payment rails. Computer-centric: yes. Source: https://stablecoininsider.org/ai-agents-for-stablecoins-in-2026/ (2026-04 data, published 2026)

13. Payment-layer guidance stresses that "spending limits, category restrictions, and time-bound authorizations aren't optional extras—they're the entire trust model," and should be built into agent authorization from day one, indicating agent operators currently lack default guardrails and must engineer them themselves. Affects: teams deploying purchasing agents. Computer-centric: yes. Source: https://eco.com/support/en/articles/14839400-what-is-agentic-commerce-the-2026-guide (2026)

14. On login/credential handling, "agents cannot access user password credentials for security reasons, and users must input passwords directly before the agent continues subsequent workflows" — a structural gap where humans must sit in the loop just to unblock login steps. Affects: people supervising computer-use agents through MFA/login walls. Computer-centric: yes. Source: search summary citing OpenAI's Computer-Using Agent documentation — https://openai.com/index/computer-using-agent/ [unverified exact wording — summarized by search tool, not directly quoted from primary source]

15. Developer time on reviewing AI-generated code now exceeds time writing it: a Q1 2026 survey of "nearly 3,000 developers found that developers now spend 11.4 hours per week reviewing AI-generated code, compared to just 9.8 hours writing new code," a reversal from 2024 when writing led by about four hours. "67 percent of developers note reviewing more AI-generated code than a year ago, while 52 percent spend more time debugging problems AI has introduced." Affects: developers supervising coding agents. Computer-centric: yes. Source: https://www.sonarsource.com/state-of-code-developer-survey-report.pdf (Q1 2026)

16. Trust in AI coding output is falling even as usage rises: "Only 29% of developers trust AI outputs to be accurate, down from 40% in 2024," and "75% report that they read every line of AI generated code," with "56% of developers report that they often need to make major changes to clean up AI code." Affects: developers supervising/correcting coding agents. Computer-centric: yes. Source: https://shiftmag.dev/state-of-code-2025-7978/ (2025)

17. Browser-hosting/sandbox costs for agent builders scale unpredictably: Browserbase's tiered plans ($0/$20/$99/custom) include base allowances with overage "charged at $0.10-0.12 per browser hour and $10-12 per GB of proxy data," and "one use case calculated $249 a month total with $150 of that for bandwidth overages," showing the sticker plan price understates real spend. Affects: teams buying browser infrastructure for agents. Computer-centric: yes. Source: https://www.tinyfish.chat/blog/browserbase-pricing (2026)

18. A cost comparison claims "at scale, Browser Use is about 5x cheaper per browser hour and about 2x cheaper per GB of proxy data compared to Browserbase," implying meaningful price dispersion and switching pressure among agent-infrastructure buyers. Affects: agent-infra buyers comparing vendors. Computer-centric: yes. Source: https://browser-use.com/posts/browser-use-vs-browserbase (2026)

19. AgentOps is described as an emerging discipline: "monitoring, tracing, evaluation pipelines, cost/latency control, and guardrails," with job postings for "AI Operations Engineer" describing work to "design, build, and run agentic workflows that automate operational tasks... shipping working automations weekly, owning them in production." Affects: teams hiring to supervise/operate agents at work. Computer-centric: yes. Source: https://agenticengineeringjobs.com/roles/agentops (2026)

20. Observability tooling has real gaps builders complain about: LangSmith has "framework lock-in... non-LangChain stacks lose most of the integration advantage," "no issue lifecycle tracking," and seat pricing "$39/seat/month on Plus" that "gets expensive with larger teams"; Langfuse (acquired by ClickHouse) has "no automatic issue clustering or eval generation" and "multi-step causal analysis in agent traces is manual." Affects: teams buying/using agent-observability tools to debug agent traces. Computer-centric: yes. Source: https://latitude.so/blog/best-llm-observability-tools-agents-latitude-vs-langfuse-langsmith (2026)

## Patterns

- **Detection/blocking arms race**: CAPTCHAs, Cloudflare bot policies, and lawsuits (Amazon v. Perplexity, Reddit v. Perplexity/Anthropic) are actively closing off agent access to sites that were previously scrapeable or usable, forcing builders into legal and technical workarounds (findings 3-10).
- **Machine-readable interfaces are thin and unevenly adopted**: llms.txt exists but adoption and crawler usage are low; MCP is more useful but young; login/credential handoff to humans remains a hard wall (findings 11, 14).
- **Payments for agents are live but guardrail-poor**: stablecoin/x402 rails are scaling in transaction volume, but spending-limit and authorization design is described as something builders must build themselves, not something platforms give by default (findings 12-13).
- **Human supervision burden is rising, not falling, for coding agents**: review time now exceeds write time, trust in output is dropping, and a majority of developers say they must rewrite agent output (findings 15-16).
- **Agent infrastructure spend is opaque and dispersed**: browser-hosting/sandbox pricing has large overage components and wide vendor price gaps, and observability tools have specific, named functional gaps (issue tracking, causal tracing, lock-in) that builders pay to work around (findings 17-20).
