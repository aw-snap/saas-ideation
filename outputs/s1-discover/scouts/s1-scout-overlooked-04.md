# Scout: overlooked-04 — Elderly people, family caregivers, disabled people, guardians/fiduciaries, death admin

## Findings

1. **63 million unpaid family caregivers, averaging 27 hrs/week.** "63 million Americans... provided ongoing care for an adult or a child with a complex medical condition or a disability in the past year" and "Caregivers spend an average of 27 hours per week providing care, and 24% provide 40 or more hours a week." Source: AARP/National Alliance for Caregiving, Caregiving in the US 2025 (July 2025), https://www.aarp.org/content/dam/aarp/ppi/topics/ltss/family-caregiving/caregiving-in-us-2025.doi.10.26419-2fppi.00373.001.pdf — affects unpaid family caregivers — computer-centric: no (time is mostly hands-on, but coordination screen work is embedded in these hours).

2. **Unpaid caregiving is now valued at $1.01 trillion/year.** "59 million caregivers of adults provided 49.5 billion hours of care in 2024 at an average value of $20.41 per hour, summing to a total economic value of $1.01 trillion." Source: AARP, "Valuing the Invaluable" 2026 update (March 2026), https://www.aarp.org/pri/topics/ltss/family-caregiving/valuing-the-invaluable-2026-update/ — affects unpaid caregivers/policy scale — computer-centric: no.

3. **Medicare Advantage prior-auth denials are widespread but rarely appealed, and mostly win when appealed.** "Medicare Advantage insurers made nearly 53 million prior authorization determinations in 2024 and denied approximately 4.1 million requests, or 7.7%... Enrollees contested only about 12% of denied authorization requests in 2024, but more than 80% of those were partially or fully overturned on appeal." Source: KFF, https://www.kff.org/medicare/medicare-advantage-insurers-made-nearly-53-million-prior-authorization-determinations-in-2024/ (2025) — affects patients and the family members who file appeals on their behalf — computer-centric: yes (portal forms, fax, phone-tree appeals).

4. **New CMS prior-auth speed rule doesn't take effect until 2026.** "Starting in 2026, Medicare Advantage plans must respond to standard prior authorization requests within 7 calendar days, down from the current 14, with urgent requests remaining at 72 hours." Source: CMS-0057-F coverage, https://www.healthcaredive.com/news/medicare-advantage-prior-authorization-requests-rise-2024-kff/810888/ (2024/2025) — affects caregivers waiting on approvals for parents' care — computer-centric: yes.

5. **Medicaid "unwinding" terminated coverage overwhelmingly for paperwork reasons, not ineligibility.** "Nearly 1.4 million people across 22 states lost Medicaid as of June 20, 2023, with 71% of this population having their coverage terminated for procedural reasons, such as incomplete paperwork and outdated contact information. Some states have reported procedural or paperwork-related terminations for more than 80% of completed determinations." Source: CBPP, Unwinding Watch, https://www.cbpp.org/research/health/unwinding-watch-tracking-medicaid-coverage-as-pandemic-protections-end (ongoing, unwinding completed by end of 2025 per CMS) — affects Medicaid enrollees and the family members handling their redetermination paperwork — computer-centric: yes (state portal forms, mailed notices, document upload).

6. **People with disabilities face extra difficulty in Medicaid recertification.** "Individuals with disabilities faced significant challenges to enrolling in Medicaid or recertifying non-MAGI eligibility due to onerous paperwork and administrative hassles" and unwinding "may have a profound effect on Medicaid enrollees with disabilities, who may have more difficulty navigating administrative and logistical hurdles." Source: PMC/NIH review, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12343369/ (2024/2025) — affects disabled Medicaid enrollees and their caregivers — computer-centric: yes.

7. **FBI IC3: elder fraud losses hit $4.9 billion in 2024, complaints up 46%.** "There was a total of $4.885 billion in losses from 147,127 complaints. This is a 46% increase in complaints from 2023, as well as a 43% increase in losses." Top categories: tech support fraud, romance scams, crypto fraud. Source: FBI IC3 2024 Elder Fraud Report via AARP summary, https://www.aarp.org/money/scams-fraud/fbi-report-fraud-2024/ (2025) — affects older adults directly, and family members who discover/clean up after the fraud — computer-centric: yes (phone/email/social-media vectors).

8. **FTC: reported senior fraud losses hit $2.4 billion in 2024 but true losses estimated far higher.** "Older adults lost $2.4 billion to fraud schemes in 2024... the FTC estimated that real losses experienced by older adults in 2024 could be as high as $82 billion" (up from "$600 million in losses in 2020"). "Credit cards (26% of loss reports) and gift cards (16% of loss reports) were the fraud payment methods most frequently reported by older adults." Source: FTC, Protecting Older Consumers 2024-2025 report, coverage at https://www.cnbc.com/2025/12/13/financial-fraud-seniors-ftc.html and https://www.aarp.org/money/scams-fraud/older-adults-ftc-fraud-report/ (December 2025) — affects older adults and the family/fiduciaries who monitor their accounts — computer-centric: yes.

9. **EVV apps used by home-health aides fail routinely at clock-in/out, costing pay.** App Store reviews of HHAeXchange: "There's at least 3/4 times a month I'll get an error message and am unable to clock in/out." "The app freezes a lot, even sometimes block your phone signal when you use." "Even if you are in the client house the gps doesn't work sometimes." One caregiver noted that when the app fails, "agencies are losing money" since workers don't get paid for hours the system can't record. Source: Apple App Store reviews for HHAeXchange, https://apps.apple.com/us/app/hhaexchange/id883673336 (accessed 2026) — affects home-health aides and the family EVV coordinates with — computer-centric: yes.

10. **EVV also depends on client home wifi, creating an awkward access problem.** "If you are not connected to stable network doesn't work, mean clients wifi internet house," requiring aides to request client wifi access. Source: same App Store review set, https://apps.apple.com/us/app/hhaexchange/id883673336 — affects home-health aides and the households whose wifi they must borrow — computer-centric: yes.

11. **Guardianship annual accounting/reporting requirements vary by state and are procedurally heavy.** Examples: Minnesota requires guardians to "file annual Personal Well-Being Reports and Affidavits of Service through the MyMNGuardian online application" (effective April 15, 2024); Texas requires "Annual Reports for calendar year 2025 must include all of 2025... and will not be accepted prior to January 1, 2026"; New York's "Annual Report, sometimes called the 'Annual Accounting,' is always due in May." Sources: Minnesota Judicial Branch, https://mncourts.gov/help-topics/guardianship/complaint-process; Texas JBCC, https://txcourts.gov/jbcc/compliance/annual-reporting/; NY Courts, https://www.nycourts.gov/guardianship-matters-elder-justice/manual-initial-annual-reports (2024-2025) — affects court-appointed guardians/conservators, often unpaid family members — computer-centric: partly (online filing portals per state, but underlying accounting is manual bookkeeping).

12. **DOJ's ADA Title II web-accessibility deadlines for government sites were just extended, delaying fixes.** "On April 20, 2026, the Department published an Interim Final Rule extending the compliance date for State and local government entities with a total population of 50,000 or more to April 26, 2027," with smaller jurisdictions pushed to April 2028; DOJ cited "resource constraints, staffing limitations, slower-than-expected technological solutions (including limits of generative artificial intelligence for remediation)." Source: Federal Register, https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/04/20/2026-07663/extension-of-compliance-dates-for-nondiscrimination-on-the-basis-of-disability-accessibility-of-web (April 2026) — affects disabled users of government benefits portals (SSA, state Medicaid, DMV) — computer-centric: yes.

13. **Website accessibility lawsuits rebounded sharply in 2025.** "Plaintiffs filed 3,117 website accessibility lawsuits in federal court in 2025 – a 27% increase from 2024," after "2,452 website accessibility lawsuits in federal court in 2024 – a 13% decrease from 2023." Website suits were "36% of the total number of ADA Title III lawsuits filed in federal court in 2025." Source: Seyfarth Shaw, ADA Title III blog, https://www.adatitleiii.com/2026/03/federal-court-website-accessibility-lawsuit-filings-bounce-back-in-2025/ (March 2026) — affects disabled users of commercial and public websites — computer-centric: yes.

14. **Older adults own smartphones at much lower rates than younger adults, a baseline access gap.** "Seventy-eight percent of people aged 65 or more own smartphones, making them the least likely age group to do so, compared with 97% of adults aged fewer than 50 years and 90% of those aged 50 to 64." Source: Pew Research, cited via AARP 2025/2026 Technology Trends reports, https://www.aarp.org/pri/topics/technology/internet-media-devices/2026-technology-trends-older-adults/ — affects older adults directly using benefits/health portals — computer-centric: yes.

15. **Caregivers already lean on tech to coordinate care, but adoption of caregiver-specific tools is still a minority.** "55% [of caregivers] using one or more forms of tech to coordinate caregiving responsibilities" while "33% are beginning to embrace tech specifically designed to assist them." Source: AARP, 2025 Technology Trends and Older Adults (caregiving supplement), https://www.ageinplacetech.com/files/aip/2025-technology-trends-older-adults-caregiving.doi_.10.26419-2fres.00891.007.pdf (2025) — affects family caregivers — computer-centric: yes.

16. **Daily money managers/professional fiduciaries are a distinct, moderately-paid role built entirely around this admin burden.** "Daily Money Managers take over someone's day-to-day financial tasks... responsibilities ranging from gathering tax information to handling ongoing bills and working with medical and insurance bills," including "monthly bill payments, tax preparation, balancing checkbooks, decoding medical bills, and negotiating with creditors." Pay: "Daily Money Managers typically earn between $44,000 and $69,000 per year." Source: ZipRecruiter/Indeed job-market aggregation, https://www.ziprecruiter.com/Jobs/Daily-Money-Manager and https://www.bedrockfiduciaries.com/daily-money-management/ (accessed 2026) — affects families who hire out this work, and the paid fiduciaries doing it — computer-centric: yes (bill pay portals, medical-bill decoding, multiple logins per client).

17. **Death admin: bank accounts freeze on notice of death and require certified paperwork to reopen/close.** "Banks typically freeze accounts as soon as they're notified of the account holder's death. The account remains frozen until the executor provides the required documentation," typically "a certified copy of the death certificate, the deceased's account information... and valid identification," sometimes plus "probate documents, the will, or proof that you're the executor." "The process of closing accounts can take anywhere from a few weeks to several months, depending on how complex the estate is." Probate itself "generally open[s] 30 to 90 days after the date of death." Source: consumer-finance aggregator citing bank policies, https://www.elayne.com/resources/how-to-close-a-bank-account-after-someone-dies (2026) — affects adult children/executors settling a parent's estate — computer-centric: yes (repeated document upload to each institution, separately).

18. **Physicians (and by extension the family members chasing the same insurers) face heavy prior-auth time costs.** "Doctors spend an average of 13 hours a week completing prior authorization paperwork," involving "multiple calls between a doctor's office and the health insurer, faxing documents." Source: aggregated coverage of AMA prior-auth survey data, cited via search results tied to https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prior_authorization (2024/2025) — [unverified, secondary summary not independently opened] — affects patients' family members who often mirror this chase from the other end — computer-centric: yes.

## Territories the evidence suggests

- Benefits and insurance paperwork (Medicaid recertification, Medicare Advantage prior-auth appeals) burns caregiver time mostly on portals, forms, faxes and phone trees, and procedural (not eligibility) failures are the biggest driver of coverage loss.
- Frontline paid aides depend on EVV apps (HHAeXchange, Sandata) that routinely fail at the core clock-in task, directly costing them pay and creating billing disputes for agencies.
- Court-supervised guardians/conservators face state-specific, deadline-driven annual accounting and reporting obligations that are still largely manual bookkeeping funneled into narrow state portals.
- Older adults and disabled people are disproportionately targeted by fraud (billions in annual losses) while also facing the worst baseline device/portal access, and government/commercial site accessibility remediation keeps getting delayed.
- Settling a parent's estate ("death admin") requires repeating the same certified-document submission separately to every bank, agency and account, with no shared record across institutions.
