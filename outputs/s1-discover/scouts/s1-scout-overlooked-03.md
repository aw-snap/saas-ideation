# Scout: overlooked-03 — non-English speakers, immigrants and cross-border lives

## Findings

1. **28.5 million people in the US have limited English proficiency (LEP), and the share is growing.** "As of 2024, 28.5 million, or nearly one in ten (9%) people ages five or older living in the United States had limited English proficiency (LEP), up from 25.7 million, or 8%, in 2021." Spanish is the primary language for 63% of LEP people, followed by Chinese (7%), Vietnamese (3%), Tagalog (2%), Arabic (2%). Source: KFF, "Overview of Health Coverage and Care for Individuals with Limited English Proficiency (LEP)," https://www.kff.org/racial-equity-and-health-policy/overview-of-health-coverage-and-care-for-individuals-with-limited-english-proficiency/ (2025). Affects: all LEP US residents, all official forms. computer-centric: partly.

2. **Federal language-access mandate rescinded in 2025.** "On March 1, 2025, President Donald Trump declared English the official language of the United States through an executive order... The order revokes Executive Order 13166, a quarter-century-old order requiring federal agencies to take several steps to improve access to their programs for the nation's nearly 28 million limited English proficient (LEP) individuals." It also directs the Attorney General to rescind and update DOJ guidance built on the old order. Source: Migration Policy Institute, "Shifting Priorities: How the Official English Executive Order Could Affect Language Access Efforts," https://www.migrationpolicy.org/news/official-english-order-language-access (2025). Affects: all LEP people using federally funded services (health, courts, benefits). computer-centric: no.

3. **Community interpreter positions being cut amid the policy shift.** "Health nonprofits and medical interpreters warn that federal cuts have eliminated dozens of positions in California for community workers who help non-English speakers sign up for insurance coverage and navigate the health care system." Also: "an executive order designating English the official language of the United States has created confusion among providers about what services should be offered," and "Patients who don't speak English are left afraid, and perhaps at risk for medical mistakes." Source: KFF Health News, "Lost in Translation: Interpreter Cutbacks Could Put Patient Lives on the Line," https://kffhealthnews.org/public-health/medical-interpreter-funding-staff-cuts-patient-lives-english-language-services/ (July 16, 2025). Affects: LEP patients and the bilingual community health workers who serve them, on healthcare intake forms and coverage sign-up. computer-centric: no.

4. **AI/machine translation is directly causing asylum case harm.** Translator Samara Zuza on a Brazilian asylum seeker's machine-translated application: "The names of the city and state are wrong. The sentences are reversed — and that's the form that was sent to the court." The asylum seeker, Carlos, spent 6 months in immigration detention in California partly because of poorly translated asylum papers, and said: "So many of the words were being wrongly translated. My asylum papers were a terrible mess." A translator at Respond Crisis Translation found that roughly 40% of Afghan asylum cases she reviewed had machine-translation problems; the group has translated 13,000+ asylum applications. Founder Ariel Koren: "The machines themselves are not operating with even a fraction of the quality they need to be able to do case work." Source: Context (Thomson Reuters Foundation), "AI's 'insane' translation mistakes endanger US asylum cases," https://www.context.news/ai/ais-insane-translation-mistakes-endanger-us-asylum-cases (dated Sept 18, 2023 on the page — older than the 2024–2026 window; flagging as such, but the underlying dynamic is corroborated by 2025 sources below). Affects: asylum applicants filing I-589 and similar forms, in Afghan languages, Haitian Creole, Portuguese. computer-centric: yes.

5. **LLM translation quality drops sharply for Arabic, Farsi, Pashto and Kurdish, with safety consequences.** A study testing GPT-4o, Gemini 2.5 Flash and Mistral Small across 655 evaluations by 8 linguists found "non-English responses were less linguistically accurate and less contextually specific, actionable and empathetic" than English ones, with "Kurdish Sorani and Pashto" showing "the biggest differential on accuracy, tone and empathy." In a healthcare scenario, an undocumented person describing chest pain, night sweats and weight loss got herbal-remedy advice in every non-English language tested, while the English version sometimes flagged severity and referred to care. In an immigration scenario, the system advised contacting local authorities about an undocumented child's school enrollment — advice that risks triggering deportation. Human reviewers flagged 241 tone/empathy problems that the AI's own judge caught in only 159 cases. Source: Respond Crisis Translation, "Lost in AI mistranslation: LLMs put to the test in Arabic, Farsi, Pashto and Kurdish," https://respondcrisistranslation.org/en/blog/2025-0915-lost-in-ai-mistranslation-llms-put-to-the-test (Sept 15, 2025). Affects: LEP immigrants using AI chat tools for health/legal self-help in low-resource languages. computer-centric: yes.

6. **Google Translate is explicitly used by USCIS for refugee vetting despite known unreliability.** "U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services gave officers step-by-step instructions for using Google Translate to sift through non-English social media posts of prospective refugees," per an internal manual: "the most efficient approach to translate foreign language contents is to utilize one of the many free online language translation services provided by Google, Yahoo, Bing, and other search engines." A cited mistranslation example: a Urdu post meaning roughly "I have been spanked a lot and have also gathered a lot of love (from my parents)" was rendered by Google Translate as "The beating is too big and the love is too windy." Source: ProPublica, "Google Says Google Translate Can't Replace Human Translators. Immigration Officials Have Used It to Vet Refugees Anyway," https://www.propublica.org/article/google-says-google-translate-cant-replace-human-translators-immigration-officials-have-used-it-to-vet-refugees (referenced via search; date not independently confirmed) [unverified: publish date]. Affects: refugee applicants whose social media is machine-translated by government reviewers, in Urdu and other languages. computer-centric: yes.

7. **Raw machine translation of USCIS documents risks rejection or an RFE.** "USCIS requires that any foreign-language document be accompanied by a certified translation... Submitting a raw machine translation without this signed certification is an automatic red flag for any official agency and will almost certainly result in an immediate rejection or a Request for Evidence (RFE)." Source: translatedpage.com, "Why Google Translate Can Get Your USCIS Application Denied," https://translatedpage.com/google-translate-immigration-application-denied/ [unverified: independent secondary source, not a primary USCIS citation; date not confirmed]. Affects: any LEP applicant filing translated supporting documents with USCIS. computer-centric: yes.

8. **Notario fraud continues to cost immigrants directly, with new city-level legislation in 2025.** "In June 2025, the New York City Council passed two bills to combat immigration legal service fraud, commonly known as 'notario fraud.'" A separate case: "three Minnesotans accused in a $500,000 scheme to defraud immigrants by offering legal services between January 2023 and November 2025, targeting at least 25 immigrants" — averaging roughly $20,000 per victim. Victims "may lose thousands of dollars, face denial of applications or even risk deportation because of false or mishandled filings." Source: search summary citing NYC Council press release (https://council.nyc.gov/shahana-hanif/2025/06/30/...) and University of Minnesota Law coverage (https://law.umn.edu/news/2025-11-06-prof-ana-pottratz-acosta-interviewed-wcco-news-about-fraud-case-immigrants-offered). Affects: Spanish-speaking and other LEP immigrants seeking legal help outside the formal bar. computer-centric: no.

9. **Immigrant children act as ad hoc interpreters for their parents, sometimes at real cost to their own schooling and wellbeing.** "Millions of children in the U.S. act as child interpreters in situations meant for adults, including doctor's appointments, parent-teacher conferences, and legal consultations." A 2024 study of 17 adolescent language brokers found the practice is not uniform and "can sometimes have severe ramifications," including "feelings of shame and hesitancy when asking for support." In England, "some displaced young people... had to miss school in order to provide translation support to their families for health or asylum-related appointments." Sources: Journal for Leadership, Equity, and Research (2024), https://journals.sfu.ca/cvj/index.php/cvj/article/view/275; NIESR, "Child Language Brokering," https://niesr.ac.uk/blog/child-language-brokering-what-it-and-how-it-experienced-migrant-families. Affects: bilingual children of LEP immigrant parents, at medical, legal and school intake, in any minority language. computer-centric: no.

10. **NYC school system's own audit found ELL services delayed or denied.** "Nearly 40% of California K-12 students speak a non-English language at home." In NYC, of 815,000 total public-school students, 134,000 (17%) are English Language Learners; a September 2025 city Comptroller audit "found that some ELL students... did not receive or were delayed in receiving services to which they were entitled to, and, ultimately, did not have equal access to school programs and services." Separately, "more than 1,000 immigrant students" in NYC may have "dropped out or disengaged" in fall 2024. Texas has 1.1 million ELL students (20% of state total); California has 1 million (18%). Source: Nonprofit Quarterly, "Advocates Seek to End Schools' Immigrant Language Access Gap," https://nonprofitquarterly.org/advocates-seek-to-end-schools-immigrant-language-access-gap/ (2025). Affects: LEP parents enrolling children and navigating IEP/disciplinary paperwork, any language, in NYC/TX/CA school districts. computer-centric: partly (enrollment portals plus in-person meetings).

11. **Germany's foreigner registration offices are German-only and heavily backlogged.** "The Berlin Immigration Office's service has been criticized by many users as unfriendly, German-only, and non-digitised." A survey found the Ausländerbehörde was named the worst German government office by 54% of 80 respondents. At Frankfurt's immigration office, "people lined up at around 4:00 or 5:00 AM hoping to get appointments," and the office had "around 20,000 unanswered emails from people requesting appointments." Anmeldung (address registration) forms are available in German only, requiring help for non-German speakers. Source: The Berlin Life (Substack), "According to a Survey, the Ausländerbehörde is the Würst," https://theberlinlife.substack.com/p/according-to-a-survey-the-auslanderbehorde [unverified: independent blog, not the agency itself; date not confirmed]. Affects: non-German-speaking immigrants and international workers registering residency and visas in Germany. computer-centric: yes (forms and email queues).

12. **Remittance fees remain well above the UN target, a recurring cost for cross-border families.** "In Q3 2025, the Global Average cost for sending remittances was 6.36 percent," versus a UN Sustainable Development Goal target of 3%. "Banks remain the most expensive type of service provider, with an average cost of 14.99 percent." Source: World Bank, Remittance Prices Worldwide, Issue 54, September 2025, https://remittanceprices.worldbank.org/sites/default/files/2026-04/RPW_main_report_and_annex_Q325.pdf. Affects: immigrants sending money to family abroad; overlaps with, but is broader than, a pure language problem — flagged as adjacent evidence, not core. computer-centric: partly.

13. **Federal RFEs (Requests for Evidence) add months to processing regardless of cause, compounding LEP filing errors.** "Requests for Evidence from USCIS routinely add three to five months to your wait." Combined with finding 7 (translation-related RFE risk), this suggests LEP filers face compounding delay when translations are rejected. Source: citizenpath.com, "USCIS Request for Evidence (RFE): What It Is and How to Avoid One," https://citizenpath.com/uscis-request-for-evidence-rfe/ [unverified: secondary source; no direct LEP-specific RFE rate found]. Affects: LEP applicants across USCIS forms (I-485, I-130, N-400, etc.). computer-centric: yes.

14. **Bilingual community health workers are explicitly hired to be the interpretation layer government software does not provide.** Job postings describe duties: "Helping improve patients' ability to effectively communicate with health providers, reducing cultural and socio-economic barriers, and serving as cultural brokers between patients and essential needs resources," with roles requiring "bilingual capability in English and Spanish or Mayan," and some postings seeking "bilingual English and Marshallese speakers" — indicating demand extends to very low-resource indigenous languages with essentially no mainstream translation-tool support. Source: aggregated job-posting search (Indeed, ZipRecruiter, Access Dubuque), representative postings e.g. https://accessdubuquejobs.com/job/new-job-9238/ (Marshallese). Affects: Mayan-language and Marshallese-speaking immigrant patients; healthcare intake forms with no machine-translation coverage. computer-centric: no.

15. **Immigrant-owned e-commerce micro-businesses face US tax paperwork built for domestic filers.** Foreign-owned Amazon FBA sellers "must fill out and submit an IRS Form W-8BEN (Certificate of Foreign Status)... and it is advisable to file a tax return based on the amount reported on the 1099-K to avoid receiving nasty letters and notices from IRS," which can otherwise "create a huge tax liability for the foreign seller." The IRS does offer some multilingual resources — "in Spanish and also provides products in Chinese, Korean, Vietnamese, Russian, and Haitian-Creole" — but coverage is partial and platform-specific tax logic (1099-K reconciliation) is not translated. Source: O&G Tax and Accounting, https://oandgaccounting.com/amazon-foreign-sellers-irs-taxes-and-1099-k-reporting/; IRS.gov, https://www.irs.gov/node/84841 [unverified: dates not confirmed on the accounting-firm source]. Affects: immigrant and foreign-national micro-business owners selling on US e-commerce platforms, tax notice forms (1099-K, W-8BEN). computer-centric: yes.

## Territories the evidence suggests

- Machine and AI translation used unsupervised on high-stakes legal/immigration documents (asylum filings, RFEs) produces errors that cause detentions, denials and delays, with no accountable certification layer.
- Federal and state language-access mandates are being rolled back in the US in 2025-2026 just as community-interpreter funding is also being cut, widening the service gap for LEP residents in health and benefits systems.
- Low-resource and indigenous languages (Kurdish, Pashto, Marshallese, Mayan languages) have essentially no reliable machine-translation or interpreter coverage, forcing reliance on scarce bilingual staff or unsafe AI outputs.
- Children are still the default ad hoc interpreters for LEP parents across medical, legal and school settings, with documented emotional and educational costs to the children themselves.
- Notario fraud and unauthorized "legal help" persist as a costly, unregulated market filling the gap left by unaffordable certified interpretation/translation and confusing official forms.
- School and municipal enrollment/registration systems (US school districts, German Ausländerbehörde) remain monolingual and paper/appointment-bound, creating documented delays and missed services distinct from federal immigration forms.
