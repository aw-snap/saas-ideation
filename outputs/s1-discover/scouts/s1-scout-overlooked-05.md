# Scout: overlooked, slice 5 — rural, agricultural, field and informal workers

## Findings

1. **FSMA 204 traceability deadline pushed to 2028, but small-farm exemption is narrow.** "The FDA originally set a compliance date of January 20, 2026... In November 2025, Congress passed the Continuing Appropriations Act of 2026 directing the FDA not to enforce the rule before July 20, 2028." Exemption: "If your farm averages $25,000 or less in annual produce sales (adjusted for inflation), you're exempt... there is no blanket small-business carve-out; if your product is on the Food Traceability List, you are covered regardless of size." Source: https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/08/07/2025-14967/requirements-for-additional-traceability-records-for-certain-foods-compliance-date-extension and https://foodready.ai/blog/fsma-204/ (2025-2026). Affects: farm/produce growers and packers. Computer-centric: yes (record-keeping systems, lot codes, data sharing with trading partners).

2. **1099-K threshold reverts to $20,000/200 transactions for 2025 tax year, reducing paperwork for casual sellers but not eliminating tax duty.** "The One Big Beautiful Bill Act of 2025 repeals the $600 threshold... returning the Form 1099-K reporting threshold to $20,000 and 200 transactions." Yet "Gig workers still need to keep track of their income because they're required to file a tax return with the IRS if they have net earnings of $400 or more from gig work, regardless of whether they receive a 1099-K form." Source: https://www.1099online.com/blog/form-1099-k-threshold/ and https://www.criadv.com/insight/gig-workers-whats-up-with-form-1099-k/ (2025-2026). Affects: informal sellers, gig workers. Computer-centric: partial (income tracking happens on phone/apps; filing is paperwork).

3. **1099-NEC/MISC threshold rises to $2,000 for 2026, changing who gets formal income documentation.** "Beginning in 2026, both Form 1099-NEC and Form 1099-MISC will have their reporting thresholds raised to $2,000." Source: https://financialsolutionadvisors.com/blog/new-reporting-requirements-for-money-earned-online/ (2025-2026). Affects: solo tradespeople, informal contractors who now may lack formal proof-of-income documents from small clients. Computer-centric: no.

4. **Owner-operators report OOIDA's own paperwork/authority services falling short.** A member "reported issues with OOIDA's handling of authority paperwork, saying they had to resolve PUC registration problems themselves after OOIDA spent 1.5 months on an email address dispute." Source: https://www.thetruckersreport.com/truckingindustryforum/threads/own-authority-paperwork.2353424/ [date on thread not confirmed, treat as recent forum post — unverified exact date]. Affects: independent truckers/owner-operators. Computer-centric: partial (authority/PUC filings are portal-based).

5. **OOIDA is actively petitioning FMCSA to change Hours of Service rules citing real-world friction.** "OOIDA has filed a petition with the FMCSA to change Hours of Service regulations... calls for allowing drivers to 'pause' their 14-hour clock for up to three straight hours during a day." Source: https://www.thetruckersreport.com/news/ooida-pushes-hours-service-rule-change/ (undated on snippet, recent). Affects: owner-operators. Computer-centric: yes (ELD logging apps enforce the clock).

6. **Only about half of US farms have broadband; roughly a fifth have no internet at all.** "A little more than half of all farms in America have access to broadband." Also: "21.3 percent of producers don't have access to the internet," while "the percentage of farm operations or operator residences with internet access increased from 75.4 percent in 2017 to 78.7 percent in 2022." Source: https://www.hoosieragtoday.com/2024/08/18/almost-one-quarter-of-farmers-dont-have-internet-access/ and https://investigatemidwest.org/2024/05/15/graphic-high-speed-internet-access-for-farmers-varies-by-state/ (2024). Affects: small farmers/ranchers. Computer-centric: no (connectivity constraint underlying any screen tool).

7. **Farmers are shifting business activity online even with patchy connectivity.** "50 percent of farms used the internet to purchase agricultural inputs, which was an increase of 18 percent from 2023" and "29 percent of farms used the internet to market agricultural activities, which was an increase of 6 percent from 2023." Source: https://esmis.nal.usda.gov (USDA NASS release cited via search), and https://www.nass.usda.gov/AgCensus/ (2024). Affects: small/mid farmers. Computer-centric: yes.

8. **Gig-driver deactivation is a recurring, high-stakes, poorly-appealable event.** "An estimated 5-8% of gig drivers face a deactivation or appeal event per year, implying 250,000–440,000 potential appeal events annually across major platforms." One driver's "income dropped from $900 to $500 in a good week" after an opaque "fraudulent activity" deactivation. Source: https://gridwise.io/blog/gig-driver-deactivation-appeal and https://wageindicator.org/what-we-do/news-stories/gig-blog/2025/platform-workers-deactivation/ (2025-2026). Affects: delivery/rideshare gig workers. Computer-centric: yes (entirely app-mediated).

9. **NYC forced reinstatement and payout over minimum-pay violations shows scale of platform-worker pay disputes.** "Uber Eats agreed to reinstate the accounts of as many as 10,000 people that were deactivated between December 2023 and September 2024... Uber Eats, Fantuan, and HungryPanda will pay a cumulative $5.2 million over violations of New York City's minimum pay law for delivery workers." Source: https://www.aol.com/articles/deactivated-accounts-one-gig-workers-113001230.html (2024-2025, exact publication date unverified). Affects: delivery gig workers. Computer-centric: yes.

10. **Gig platforms mostly force arbitration, but small claims remains a narrow escape route.** "Most gig platforms require mandatory arbitration through their terms of service... However, small claims court is usually exempt from arbitration agreements for most gig platforms, and if your case is under your state's limit ($5,000 to $25,000 depending on where you live), you can skip arbitration and go straight to court." Source: https://terms.law/FAQ/gig-economy/deactivation-appeals-faq.html [unverified — legal-explainer site, not a primary regulatory source] (2026). Affects: gig workers. Computer-centric: no (dispute resolution off-platform).

11. **EU Platform Work Directive creates an employment presumption, with national transposition still pending.** "Directive (EU) 2024/2831 on improving working conditions in platform work was adopted on 14 October 2024... Member States have until 2 December 2026 to transpose it into national law... It creates a presumption of employment when digital platforms exercise direction and control over workers, placing the burden of proof on platforms." Source: https://cms.law/en/bel/legal-updates/from-gig-to-guarantee-how-the-eu-is-transforming-platform-work and https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/policies/platform-work-eu/ (2024-2025). Affects: EU gig/platform workers. Computer-centric: partial (algorithm-transparency provisions concern app-side data).

12. **Nannies/domestic workers commonly lack formal pay stubs, blocking loans and rentals — a documented gap households are told to fill manually.** "Pay stubs are especially important when your nanny needs a loan or wants to rent an apartment and needs evidence of income" and "many states impose an obligation on employers to provide pay stubs to employees, including domestic workers." Source: https://www.thepaystubs.com/blog/paystubs/make-pay-stub-for-nanny and https://paystub.org/posts/nanny-pay-stub [unverified dates — commercial blog content, no clear publication date]. Affects: nannies/domestic workers and their household employers. Computer-centric: partial (record produced on paper or via a generator tool, used for offline proof).

13. **Household-employer nanny payroll is a paid-tool category, at real dollar cost for a tiny "business."** "Nest Payroll offers full-service nanny payroll for all 50 states, including tax filing, pay stubs, and W-2s for $42 per month." Source: https://nestpayroll.com/ [unverified date, product page]. Affects: domestic workers and the households employing them. Computer-centric: yes.

14. **Facebook Marketplace informal-seller fraud and dispute exposure is large and growing.** "Over 79% of Facebook Marketplace users have encountered fraud on the platform, with financial scams surging 340% in Q2 2025," and "buyers and sellers should beware of communicating or receiving payments outside of Facebook's official channels, as Facebook's Purchase Protection policies only cover payments made through Facebook Checkout." Also, "individual sellers cannot dispute chargebacks, and Seller Protection is currently only available in the US and limited to items with a sale price of $500 or less." Source: https://www.underpriced.app/blog/facebook-marketplace-scams-to-avoid-2026 and https://getmozo.com/blog/facebook-marketplace-scams-dispute-fraudulent-charges [dates partly unverified, aggregator/blog sources; treat stat with caution] (2025-2026). Affects: informal sellers on Facebook Marketplace/WhatsApp-adjacent commerce. Computer-centric: yes.

15. **FSA disaster-aid paperwork burden is documented in minutes-per-form terms, but application still requires in-person financial documentation.** "Producers should apply at their local FSA office within 8 months of the disaster designation, bringing financial records, documentation of losses, and evidence that commercial credit isn't available." Separately, official Paperwork Reduction Act notices for some FSA collections state "the public reporting burden for one FSA collection is estimated to average 6 minutes per response, while another collection is estimated to average 3 minutes per response" [unverified — this range came from an aggregated search summary, not a single confirmed source document]. Source: https://www.fema.gov/assistance/businesses/agriculture and https://www.fsa.usda.gov/resources/disaster-recovery/20232024-supplemental-disaster-assistance (2024-2025). Affects: farmers/ranchers hit by disaster. Computer-centric: no (in-person, paper-heavy).

16. **American Relief Act of 2025 pushed over $30B in disaster aid to farmers, with a hard program close date.** "The American Relief Act of 2025, signed into law on Dec. 21, 2024, provided the U.S. Department of Agriculture with more than $30 billion to deliver disaster recovery assistance to farmers and livestock producers," and "both stages of the Supplemental Disaster Relief Program (SDRP) close September 30, 2026." Source: https://farmersnavigator.com/programs/disaster-assistance (2025-2026). Affects: farmers/ranchers. Computer-centric: no.

17. **Day-laborer wage-tracking apps exist but rely on manual worker self-reporting and a nonprofit backend, not integration with employers.** "Jornaler@ is an app and service that lets day laborers track their work hours, and also to report abusive employers... Workers can easily punch in and out, and keep track of other information like vehicle model and license plate, hourly wage, work license, location, and breaks taken, with information stored both on the employee's phone and on a central server at an NDLON network worker center." Source: https://www.ilr.cornell.edu/worker-institute/blog/news/new-smartphone-app-helps-day-laborers-report-abuse-fight-wage-theft [unverified date — appears to be an older Cornell post still linked/cited in current searches, likely pre-2024; flag as possibly outdated]. Affects: day laborers. Computer-centric: yes.

18. **Mobile usage gap persists even where coverage exists, driven by affordability and skills, not signal — directly relevant to rural/informal workers' software access.** "96% of the global population living in areas with mobile internet coverage available to them, [yet] 3.1 billion people are still not using it... the focus is on addressing barriers beyond access, such as handset affordability and digital skills." Also, "adults living in rural areas in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs) are 25% less likely to use mobile internet services than those in urban areas." Source: https://www.gsma.com/newsroom/press-release/gsma-calls-for-renewed-focus-on-closing-the-usage-gap-as-more-than-3-billion-people-remain-offline-despite-available-mobile-internet-services/ (2025). Affects: rural farmers, informal workers globally. Computer-centric: no.

## Patterns seen

- Paperwork and proof-of-income requirements are shifting under regulators' feet (1099-K, 1099-NEC/MISC, FSMA 204, EU Platform Work Directive), leaving workers and micro-employers unsure which threshold or deadline currently applies.
- Where the work does land on a screen (ELD logs, gig apps, FSA portals, disaster-aid uploads), the pain is opaque decisions (deactivation, fraud flags) with weak or arbitration-gated appeal paths.
- Off-platform, informal cash/DM-based commerce (Facebook Marketplace, WhatsApp selling, day labor, domestic work) has almost no built-in dispute resolution or income documentation, forcing manual workarounds (paper stubs, screenshots).
- Rural and informal workers face a compounding gap: even where mobile coverage exists, affordability, literacy, and device constraints keep usage low, and roughly one in five US farms still lacks internet entirely.
- Government disaster and program aid keeps increasing in dollar volume but still requires in-person, paper-heavy documentation at the point of application.
