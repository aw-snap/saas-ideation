# Scout: overlooked-scout-01 — the lone back-office in tiny public and nonprofit organizations

## Findings

1. **Automatic loss of tax-exempt status after three missed filings.** "Automatic revocation occurs when an exempt organization that is required to file an annual return (e.g., Form 990, 990-EZ or 990-PF) or submit an annual electronic notice (Form 990-N, or e-Postcard) does not do so for three consecutive years." Related sources add this "usually happens without warning and is not appealable." Source: https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/automatic-revocation-of-exemption-for-nonfiling-overview (accessed 2026) and https://www.501c3.org/501c3-status-revoked/. Affects: whoever files (or forgets to file) the 990/990-N, typically a volunteer treasurer or part-time bookkeeper. Computer-centric: yes (e-Postcard portal, IRS EO search).

2. **No aggregate IRS count of revocations is published on the overview page itself** — the IRS site defines the mechanism but does not surface a running tally in-page. Source: https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/automatic-revocation-of-exemption-for-nonfiling-overview [unverified further statistics beyond definition]. Affects: researchers/small-org boards trying to gauge risk. Computer-centric: yes.

3. **Multi-state charity registration has no unified system.** "Because each state built its own law separately, there's no single federal standard governing charitable solicitation — each state sets its own registration forms, fee schedule, renewal cycle, financial reporting thresholds, and exemptions." Source: https://www.fundraisingregistration.com/about/news/what-you-need-to-know-about-multi-state-registration-for-charitable-solicitations/ (2025/2026 content). Also: "Thirty-nine states and the District of Columbia each require some form of periodic charity registration." Source: https://www.councilofnonprofits.org/running-nonprofit/governance-leadership/state-filing-requirements-nonprofits. Affects: one-person development/admin staff filing in every state they fundraise from. Computer-centric: yes — each state's own filing portal.

4. **The old attempt to unify state registration has been abandoned as unmaintained.** "The Unified Registration Statement was initiated over 10 years ago... however, it has not been maintained as states change their registration requirements and therefore the Unified Registration Statement is no longer useful." Source: https://www.fundraisingregistration.com/about/news/what-you-need-to-know-about-multi-state-registration-for-charitable-solicitations/. Affects: multi-state nonprofit admins. Computer-centric: yes.

5. **QuickBooks (desktop/business-built) is a common but ill-fitting default for churches.** "It is a desktop accounting system built for small and midsize businesses, not churches... QuickBooks was built around profit and loss, invoices, sales receipts, and expenses, which don't align perfectly with church-specific needs." Some churches report month-end closes of up to 40 hours before switching tools. Source: https://www.getkleercard.com/blogs/quickbooks-for-churches-what-it-does-well-and-what-it-misses and https://grainledger.com/blog/quickbooks-desktop-pro-2024 (2024–2026 content). Affects: church secretary/treasurer doing fund accounting in general-ledger software not built for donor restrictions. Computer-centric: yes.

6. **Churches switch away from QuickBooks to purpose-built tools like ChurchTrac**, citing the mismatch above. Source: https://www.churchtrac.com/blog/should-my-church-use-quickbooks. Affects: church admin staff. Computer-centric: yes.

7. **Small HOA software users report rigid, hard-to-customize reporting and unreliable support.** PayHOA review (HOA President, "Ama K", real estate org, 2–10 employees, posted 2026-07-15): "Many reports are rigid and offer limited customization, lacks advanced sorting filters." Another reviewer ("Ivy L", owner, posted 2026-08-04, 4.0 stars): "I scheduled several calls, but no one attended or followed up." Source: https://www.capterra.com/p/146693/PayHOA/reviews/. Affects: volunteer HOA board members / community managers. Computer-centric: yes.

8. **Ransomware directly disables small-town clerk/administrative operations.** Southold, NY (Long Island) town servers were hit by ransomware; the town's email system failed around 7:30 a.m. on a Monday, prompting IT to "unplug everything so the problem couldn't spread." Town Supervisor Al Krupski said: "If we have to go back to pen and paper, we will do whatever is necessary to keep it open." Published 2025-11-24. Source: https://northforksun.com/southold-officials-investigating-cyber-incident-affecting-towns-servers/. Affects: town clerk's office, water billing, connection to state systems. Computer-centric: yes.

9. **Ransomware against local governments is accelerating.** "The first half of 2025 alone saw a 65% increase in ransomware incidents affecting government bodies compared to the same period a year earlier." A separate 2025 analysis of 525 ransomware attacks on U.S. government entities (2018–late 2024, mostly local governments, counties, school districts and utilities) found an average ransom payment of about $872,656 per attack. Source: https://warrenaverett.com/insights/three-lessons-that-all-organizations-can-learn-from-recent-ransomware-attacks-on-municipalities/ [figures as reported by search summary; recommend independent verification — marked unverified for exact wording] `[unverified]`. Affects: small municipal offices with no dedicated IT staff. Computer-centric: yes.

10. **Small/micro nonprofits carry disproportionate technology risk.** "Smaller organizations (by staff and budget) report higher technology risk across all categories, with micro-sized nonprofits with fewer than one full-time employee triggering risk flags on 65% of survey answers, compared to just 34% among organizations with 150+ staff." Also: "organizations with no tech staff averaged nearly 60% risk flags, while the rate among organizations with more than ten tech staff dropped to 28%." Source: NTEN/Heller Consulting, "2024 Nonprofit Digital Investments Report," https://word.nten.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/2024-Nonprofit-Digital-Investments-Report.pdf (2024). Affects: sole admin/volunteer staff at micro nonprofits. Computer-centric: yes.

11. **Nonprofits cite time and money, not desire, as the blocker on technology.** "Respondents were most concerned about having enough time (54% no), then money (47% no)." Also "45% of nonprofits indicated they weren't spending enough on technology, citing lack of budget (77%), lack of funder support (47%) and lack of donor support (38%)." Source: NTEN 2024 Nonprofit Digital Investments Report, https://www.nten.org/posts/publication/2024-nonprofit-digital-investments-report. Affects: whoever is the de facto IT/admin person in a small nonprofit. Computer-centric: yes.

12. **Grant reporting burden, while reportedly declining industry-wide, remains a top administrative demand.** "Grant management is one of the most demanding administrative functions in the nonprofit sector and is almost always under-resourced. Reporting, finance review, and document collection take more time than expected." A separate figure (Center for Effective Philanthropy, cited secondhand) states grantee partners spent an average of 15 hours on reporting in 2017, down to about 5 hours by 2023 `[unverified — secondary citation, original CEP report not opened]`. Source: https://www.instrumentl.com/blog/grant-management-guide and search-summarized CEP figure. Affects: grants coordinator/part-time development staff. Computer-centric: yes.

13. **Part-time church administrator/bookkeeper roles are explicitly paid to do this screen work at modest rates.** A posting requires "10 to 15 hours per week at $20/hour minimum," with duties including "all bookkeeping functions, including paying bills and processing payroll through QuickBooks Online." Source: search summary of ZipRecruiter/Indeed listings, https://www.ziprecruiter.com/Jobs/Church-Bookkeeper and https://www.indeed.com/q-part-time-church-bookkeeping-jobs.html (2025–2026 postings). Affects: church bookkeepers paid a modest hourly rate for compliance-heavy screen work. Computer-centric: yes.

14. **Full-time church administrator median salary reported around $74,000/year**, but duties span far beyond bookkeeping — "coordinating church events, overseeing facility maintenance, managing financial records, handling communications, and supporting the pastoral staff," plus "processing payroll for church staff and handling tax-related documentation, tracking donations and generating contribution statements for members, and overseeing accounts payable and receivable." Source: Glassdoor salary data summarized via https://theleadpastor.com/career/church-administration-job-description/ and https://www.indeed.com/hire/job-description/church-administrator (2026). Affects: the single admin juggling many unrelated screen-based duties at once. Computer-centric: yes.

15. **Volunteer/board treasurer time commitments for financial oversight are commonly listed in the 3–10 hours/week or 4–7 hours/month range** across multiple posted roles, with duties described as "reviewing monthly financial statements, leading annual budgeting processes, and overseeing compliance with nonprofit financial standards" and "maintaining receipts, donations, and expense tracking using spreadsheets." Source: aggregated Idealist.org volunteer treasurer listings, e.g., https://www.idealist.org/en/volunteer-opportunity/69c4cc60dbc543c6bc3e8eece783b930-nonprofit-leadership-organization-treasurer-growing-gardens-inc-south-point (dates on listings not confirmed) `[unverified — aggregate search summary, individual listing pages not opened]`. Affects: volunteer treasurers doing spreadsheet-based bookkeeping. Computer-centric: yes.

16. **Volunteer fire departments face duplicate-entry, error-kickback burdens in incident reporting.** "Volunteer fire departments commonly complain about duplicate entry, officers getting reports kicked back over coding mistakes, and administrative staff chasing missing reports at the end of the month," and "most departments treat NFIRS fire reporting like a compliance chore, rather than viewing it as a strategic tool." NFIRS compliance is also "a regular requirement for fire departments applying for federal grants." Source: https://blog.resgrid.com/nfirs-fire-reporting/ (2026) and https://www.responserack.com/nfirs/. Affects: volunteer administrative staff at fire departments who compile and correct incident reports monthly. Computer-centric: yes.

17. **FEMA/USFA itself frames NFIRS as a federal paperwork burden it is trying to reduce**, per a 2022 Federal Register notice for the "Proposed Collection; Comment Request; National Fire Incident Reporting System (NFIRS) Version 5.0," which references "the reduction in burden hours associated with this collection due to modernization." Source: https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2022/12/09/2022-26766/agency-information-collection-activities-proposed-collection-comment-request-national-fire-incident (2022 — older than the 2024–2026 preferred window, flagged as such). Affects: volunteer/paid administrative staff filing NFIRS reports. Computer-centric: yes.

18. **A funder-side scholarly article documents that funders' own choices about application/reporting formats drive nonprofit administrative burden**, i.e., the burden is partly externally imposed and not fully within a small org's control. Source: "Challenges, Choices and Common Applications: Funders' Exercise of Power and Its Consequences for Nonprofits' Administrative Burden," https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/nvsm.70064 (2025) `[unverified — abstract-level, full text not opened]`. Affects: grants/finance staff at small nonprofits responding to varied funder formats. Computer-centric: yes.

## Territories the evidence suggests

- Compliance-deadline tracking and filing across fragmented, API-less government and regulator portals (federal 990/e-Postcard, dozens of separate state charity-registration systems) where a single missed cycle triggers irreversible loss of tax-exempt status.
- Fund/restricted-donation accounting forced into general-ledger small-business software (QuickBooks and similar) that does not model donor restrictions, producing extremely long manual month-end closes for churches and small nonprofits.
- Basic cybersecurity hygiene (backups, account offboarding, phishing/ransomware resilience) for one-person or volunteer-run local-government and nonprofit offices with no IT staff, where a single ransomware incident can force a return to "pen and paper."
- Incident/activity reporting into rigid government schemas (NFIRS) that produces duplicate data entry and repeated error-driven rework for volunteer fire department administrators.
- Reconciling and repackaging the same financial/program data into many different, incompatible funder- and state-mandated report formats.
- Low willingness/ability to pay combined with software vendors sized for larger organizations, leaving small HOA/community-association admins with rigid, unresponsive, or costly tools.
