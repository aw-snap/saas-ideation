# Scout weak-signals-04: failure modes created by AI

## Findings

1. **AI-hallucinated citations in court filings, growing weekly**: A public tracker run by researcher Damien Charlotin recorded roughly 1,490 decisions worldwide (1,000+ in the US) as of May 2026, and a separate 2026 review cites 1,148+ documented US cases, implying close to 8 new cases per day at the fastest point. Judges, clerks and opposing counsel absorb the cost of catching fabricated case law.
   - Evidence: "roughly 1,490 decisions worldwide... and more than 1,000 in the US as of May 2026" / "1,148+ documented cases of AI-related hallucinations by lawyers tracked in a U.S. database"
   - Cleanup work: clerks and opposing counsel manually verify every citation in a brief against real case databases before responding; courts now issue standing orders requiring GenAI-use disclosure.
   - Source: https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en-us/knowledge/publications/792d8bf3/ai-in-litigation-update-on-gen-ai-sanctions-in-2026 (Jan-Apr 2026)
   - computer-centric: yes

2. **Escalating court sanctions for fabricated citations**: Penalties rose from four-figure fines in 2023 to $15,000-per-attorney sanctions in 2026, plus disqualification and bar referrals in some cases.
   - Evidence: "San Diego attorney Stephen Brigandi and Portland attorney Tim Murphy were sanctioned a combined $110,000" / "a Sixth Circuit panel sanctioned two attorneys whose briefs contained more than two dozen fabricated citations, ordering each to pay $15,000"
   - Cleanup work: courts hold separate sanctions hearings, order CLE retraining, and refer attorneys to state bars for discipline.
   - Source: https://gc.ai/blog/ai-hallucination-legal-cases and https://www.sternekessler.com/news-insights/insights/ai-ip-year-in-reviewai-hallucinations-in-court-filings-and-orders-a-2025-review-of-sanctions-across-the-courts-and-rule-proposals/ (2025-2026)
   - computer-centric: yes

3. **AI-generated "slop" bug reports overwhelmed curl's security team**: curl's confirmed-vulnerability rate fell and submission volume spiked to 8x normal by July 2025, leading the project to shut down its HackerOne bug bounty at the end of January 2026.
   - Evidence: "By July 2025, cURL's submission volume spiked to 'eight times' the normal rate" / "in six years of monitoring submissions generated by AI alone, not a single one discovered a genuine vulnerability—zero" / "cURL received 20 submissions in the first 21 days of 2026; zero legitimate vulnerabilities were found"
   - Cleanup work: maintainer Daniel Stenberg and a seven-person volunteer team manually triage each low-quality report to distinguish real bugs from AI-fabricated ones, then ban repeat offenders.
   - Source: https://www.bugcrowd.com/blog/hacker-opinion-piece-how-lazy-hacking-killed-curls-bug-bounty/ (2026); https://www.theregister.com/2025/05/07/curl_ai_bug_reports/ (May 2025)
   - computer-centric: yes

4. **Maintainer quote on being "DDoSed" by AI reports**: curl's Daniel Stenberg described the flood of AI-generated vulnerability reports as a denial-of-service attack on his time.
   - Evidence: "We are effectively being DDoSed. If we could, we would charge them for this waste of our time." / "We now ban every reporter instantly who submits reports we deem AI slop."
   - Cleanup work: instant-ban policy introduced May 2025 (later found insufficient), full bounty shutdown announced January 22, 2026.
   - Source: https://www.theregister.com/2025/05/07/curl_ai_bug_reports/ (May 2025)
   - computer-centric: yes

5. **Industry-wide open-source strain from AI submissions, not just curl**: HackerOne itself acknowledged a broader pattern; Python maintainer Seth Larson flagged the same issue in December 2024.
   - Evidence: "HackerOne acknowledged receiving 'increased volume in AI-helped reports across the entire security industry'" / "Python maintainer Seth Larson raised comparable concerns in December 2024, noting that low-quality submissions create stress and contribute to open source developer burnout."
   - Cleanup work: individual project maintainers (mostly unpaid volunteers) each build their own triage/ban rules since no shared industry tooling exists yet.
   - Source: https://socket.dev/blog/curl-shuts-down-bug-bounty-program-after-flood-of-ai-slop-reports (2026)
   - computer-centric: yes

6. **AI crawler traffic overwhelming small open-source infrastructure**: Read the Docs cut traffic by 75% (800GB/day to 200GB/day) and saved about $1,500/month after blocking AI crawlers; GNOME's GitLab saw 97% bot traffic in a sample window; diaspora's servers received 70% of total traffic from AI companies.
   - Evidence: "Blocking all AI crawlers reduced traffic by 75% (800GB/day → 200GB/day)... Monthly savings: ~$1,500" / "GNOME GitLab: 97% of traffic identified as bot traffic (81,000 requests in 2.5 hours, only 3% passed proof-of-work challenge)" / "70% of total web traffic from AI companies" (diaspora)
   - Cleanup work: infrastructure admins deploy proof-of-work challenges (e.g., Anubis), manually tune rate limits, and rotate blocklists as crawlers spoof user agents and rotate IPs every ~6 hours.
   - Source: https://thelibre.news/foss-infrastructure-is-under-attack-by-ai-companies/ (2025)
   - computer-centric: yes

7. **AI crawlers ignore robots.txt and evade blocks**: crawler behavior documented as adversarial, not just heavy.
   - Evidence: "Crawlers ignore robots.txt, use randomized IP addresses and spoofed user agents, revisit sites every 6 hours, and switch identities when rate-limited or blocked."
   - Cleanup work: small-site and self-hosted infra operators (volunteers at SourceHut, KDE GitLab) manually diagnose outages caused by scraping; SourceHut had severe outages and KDE GitLab was temporarily inaccessible in March 2025.
   - Source: https://thelibre.news/foss-infrastructure-is-under-attack-by-ai-companies/ (March 2025)
   - computer-centric: yes

   - Cleanup work: incident responders and retailer security teams investigate skimmer infections and credential exposure across 100+ compromised sites; government IT teams patch and audit exposed portals after autonomous-agent probing.
