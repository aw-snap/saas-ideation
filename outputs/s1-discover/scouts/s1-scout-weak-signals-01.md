# Scout weak-signals-01: non-US regulations and deadlines

## Findings

1. **EU sustainability reporting (CSRD) scope cut and delayed via Omnibus I**: only companies with >1,000 employees and >€450m turnover must file CSRD sustainability reports; listed SMEs are fully excluded. Firms formerly in "wave two/three" that no longer meet the new thresholds drop out; those still in scope start reporting from FY27 (filed 2028).
   - Evidence: "only companies with more than 1,000 employees and a turnover of more than 450 million euros will have to submit a sustainability report in accordance with the CSRD" / Directive (EU) 2026/470, published in the Official Journal 26 Feb 2026, in force 18 Mar 2026.
   - Screen or work affected: structured sustainability disclosure filings (ESRS taxonomy/XBRL-style reporting).
   - Source: https://kpmg-law.de/en/first-omnibus-regulation-to-relax-the-obligations-of-the-csddd-csrd-and-eu-taxonomy/ ; https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2026/02/24/council-signs-off-simplification-of-sustainability-reporting-and-due-diligence-requirements-to-boost-eu-competitiveness/

2. **CSDDD (supply-chain due diligence) scope shrunk and pushed to 2029**: only companies with >5,000 employees and >€1.5bn turnover must comply, a further one-year delay from the earlier timeline. Removes most mid-size supply-chain due-diligence burden.
   - Evidence: "Only companies with more than 5,000 employees and EUR 1.5bn net turnover will need to comply with the CSDDD starting in 2029, a delay of a further year."
   - Screen or work affected: supplier due-diligence registers/evidence packs (now largely out of scope for small/mid firms).
   - Source: https://kpmg-law.de/en/first-omnibus-regulation-to-relax-the-obligations-of-the-csddd-csrd-and-eu-taxonomy/

3. **UK Companies House mandatory identity verification for directors/PSCs**: rollout began 18 November 2025 for new directors/PSCs/LLP members; existing directors and PSCs get a 12-month transition to 18 November 2026, but must verify before filing their next confirmation statement, which can fall much earlier.
   - Evidence: "Mandatory identity verification began on 18 November 2025 for new directors, new PSCs, new LLP members and overseas company directors... you have until 18 November 2026 to complete verification, but you must verify before filing the company's next confirmation statement."
   - Screen or work affected: GOV.UK One Login identity-verification portal (10–20 min self-service, or via Authorised Corporate Service Provider).
   - Source: https://www.gov.uk/government/news/companies-house-confirms-identity-verification-rollout-from-18-november-2025 ; https://www.prosolve.uk/guides/companies-house-id-verification

4. **Making Tax Digital for Income Tax (MTD IT) live from April 2026**: sole traders and landlords with qualifying income above £50,000 must keep digital records and file quarterly updates plus a year-end digital return through MTD-compatible software; threshold falls to £30,000 in 2027 and £20,000 in 2028, sharply widening who is newly in scope.
   - Evidence: "864,000 sole traders and landlords face new MTD reporting rules from April 2026" / "The threshold is due to fall to £30,000 in 2027 and £20,000 in 2028."
   - Screen or work affected: quarterly digital submissions via MTD-recognised software (replacing a single annual paper/online return).
   - Source: https://www.bytestart.co.uk/news-insights/864000-sole-traders-and-landlords-face-new-mtd-reporting-rules-from-april-2026/ ; https://www.gov.uk/guidance/find-out-if-and-when-you-need-to-use-making-tax-digital-for-income-tax

5. **NIS2 cybersecurity duties partly relaxed for micro/small firms via January 2026 amendment**: the Commission proposed targeted amendments (20 Jan 2026) to ease compliance for tens of thousands of companies while transposition across most member states completed through 2025–2026, years after the original October 2024 deadline.
   - Evidence: "the Commission proposed targeted amendments to the NIS2 directive to increase legal clarity. The amendments will ease compliance for 28,700 companies, including 6,200 micro and small-sized enterprises." / original transposition deadline 17 Oct 2024, infringement proceedings opened against 23 member states.
   - Screen or work affected: incident-reporting portal duties — early warning within 24 hours, full notification within 72 hours, final report within one month.
   - Source: https://ecs-org.eu/policy/nis2-directive-transposition-tracker/ ; https://www.globalpolicywatch.com/2026/01/germany-transposes-nis-2-directive-increased-cybersecurity-requirements-for-businesses/

6. **France B2B e-invoicing mandate confirmed for September 2026, no further postponement**: large and mid-size taxpayers must issue structured e-invoices via certified platforms (Plateformes Agréées) from 1 Sept 2026 and all companies must be able to receive them from that date; small/micro enterprises get an issuing obligation from 1 Sept 2027.
   - Evidence: "The DGFiP has confirmed that mandatory e-invoicing and e-reporting will begin on September 1, 2026, with no further postponements." / small businesses "issuing obligation starting September 1, 2027."
   - Screen or work affected: structured e-invoice formats submitted through certified private platforms (a new file-format and portal duty, not a paper/PDF process).
   - Source: https://www.ey.com/en_gl/technical/tax-alerts/french-government-announces-simplification-measures-as-part-of-september-2026-e-invoicing-mandate ; https://www.truecommerce.com/en-gb/blog/e-invoicing-in-france-a-guide-to-the-french-mandate/

7. **European Accessibility Act (EAA) enforcement live since 28 June 2025, micro-enterprises formally exempt but pressure is spreading**: businesses selling digital products/services in the EU had to make websites and interfaces WCAG-accessible by 28 June 2025; enforcement action followed within days.
   - Evidence: "Within days, French disability advocacy organizations issued formal legal notices to four major grocery retailers, demanding their e-commerce platforms meet accessibility standards by September 2025." / fines "up to €100,000 per violation."
   - Screen or work affected: website/e-commerce interface accessibility (keyboard navigation, screen-reader support) — a direct screen-level compliance duty for any firm above the micro-enterprise threshold (<10 employees, <€2m balance sheet) selling into the EU.
   - Source: https://www.insideglobaltech.com/2025/06/10/european-accessibility-act-june-2025-deadline-has-arrived/ ; https://www.siteimprove.com/blog/european-accessibility-act-what-june-2025-deadline-means/

8. **India's DPDP Rules notified 14 November 2025, phased duties through May 2027, no small-business exemption**: consent-manager framework due to be operationalised June–August 2026; full compliance required by 13 May 2027 (18 months after notification). The Act applies "regardless of size."
   - Evidence: "The Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025 were notified on November 14, 2025... Full compliance is required by May 13, 2027 — 18 months from the November 2025 notification." / "There are no blanket exemptions for small businesses."
   - Screen or work affected: consent-manager integration, breach-notification and data-principal-request handling (new digital workflows for any org processing Indian personal data).
   - Source: https://www.india-briefing.com/news/india-dpdp-compliance-timeline-enforcement-2026-27-44740.html/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_Personal_Data_Protection_Rules,_2025

9. **EU AI Act Article 4 "AI literacy" obligation already in force, enforcement window opens 2 August 2026, no size threshold**: any organisation deploying an AI system — including a sole trader using an AI assistant for client emails — is a "deployer" under Article 4 and must ensure sufficient AI literacy among staff.
   - Evidence: "The AI literacy obligation under Article 4 applies since 2 February 2025 and is enforced from 2 August 2026... the AI Act sets no size threshold for Article 4, and a sole trader using an AI assistant for client emails is a deployer and the obligation applies."
   - Screen or work affected: not screen-bound itself (a training/documentation duty), but tied to whatever AI tool the small org already operates on-screen.
   - Source: https://wtlgovernance.com/insights/updates/eu-ai-act-article-4-ai-literacy-enforcement-deadline/ ; https://learnframe.com/blog/ai-act-article-4-literacy-deadline

10. **EU AI Act high-risk obligations partially deferred but GPAI transparency duties held firm at 2 August 2026**: Articles 9–17 (provider) and Article 26 (deployer) duties for high-risk systems bind from 2 Aug 2026; standalone high-risk systems (Annex III — hiring, credit scoring, education) deferred to 2 Dec 2027; AI embedded in regulated products (Annex I) deferred to 2 Aug 2028. Transparency duties (Article 50) and GPAI enforcement were not delayed.
    - Evidence: "August 2, 2026 is the binding enforcement date for high-risk AI system obligations... high-risk obligations for stand-alone systems (Annex III...) are deferred to 2 December 2027... transparency duties (Article 50) and the AI Office's enforcement powers over general-purpose AI (GPAI) providers took effect exactly as scheduled on 2 August 2026."
    - Screen or work affected: conformity documentation, transparency labelling for AI-generated content, deployer logging obligations.
    - Source: https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-eu-ai-act-high-risk-compliance-deadline-20/ ; https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/04/us-companies-face-eu-ai-acts-possible-august-2026-compliance-deadline

11. **CSRD "Stop the Clock" directive retroactively postponed reporting for wave-two/three companies by two years before the scope cut**: companies that would have had to report from FY2025 or FY2026 got a two-year postponement even before the later scope reduction, showing a pattern of repeated deadline slippage rather than a single fixed date.
    - Evidence: "The postponement of the reporting obligation under the CSRD for companies that would normally have had to report from the 2025 or 2026 financial year by two years was already approved earlier."
    - Screen or work affected: sustainability-reporting software/registers (obligation removed for most, but firms that built processes toward the original dates absorbed sunk compliance cost).
    - Source: https://kpmg-law.de/en/first-omnibus-regulation-to-relax-the-obligations-of-the-csddd-csrd-and-eu-taxonomy/

12. **UK Companies House non-compliance is a criminal offence with financial penalty**: directors/PSCs who miss identity verification commit an offence.
    - Evidence: "If you do not comply with identity verification requirements on time, you'll be committing an offence, with a range of consequences, including a financial penalty."
    - Screen or work affected: GOV.UK One Login verification portal, tied to confirmation-statement filing deadlines that vary per company.
    - Source: https://www.simplybusiness.co.uk/knowledge/business-structure/companies-house-business-identity-verification/

13. **MTD IT replaces one annual return with five digital submissions a year**: quarterly updates plus a year-end digital return, all via MTD-recognised software, with narrow exemptions (age, disability, lack of internet access, religious belief).
    - Evidence: "Instead of one annual tax return, MTD requires quarterly updates plus one digital tax return each year, all submitted through MTD-ready software." / exemption grounds: "age, disability or health or lack of internet access, or religious beliefs that are incompatible with electronic communication."
    - Screen or work affected: recurring quarterly filing through third-party MTD software (no free universal HMRC portal for this duty — landlords/sole traders must buy or adopt compatible software).
    - Source: https://kirkwoodwilson.co.uk/making-tax-digital-2026-a-practical-guide-for-sole-traders-and-landlords-with-50k-income/ ; https://morganreach.com/making-tax-digital-mtd-in-2026/

## Territories the evidence suggests

- Recurring, software-mediated micro-filings for the smallest legal entities: UK sole traders/landlords now face quarterly digital tax submissions (MTD IT) with narrow, manual exemption routes — a repeating screen-bound burden that scales down to individuals, not just firms.
- One-time but hard-deadline identity/format transitions that trip up unprepared small orgs: Companies House ID verification and France's e-invoicing format shift both convert a previously informal or paper-based step into a mandatory portal or structured-file action with penalty risk.
- Diffuse, non-size-gated AI and data-protection duties landing on solo operators: EU AI Act Article 4 (AI literacy) and India's DPDP both apply "regardless of size," creating training/documentation obligations for one-person businesses that have never had a compliance function.
- Whiplash from EU simplification: CSRD/CSDDD scope has been cut and pushed back twice in under two years, leaving firms that built toward earlier thresholds with orphaned compliance work and firms still unsure whether looming dates (NIS2 amendments, EAA enforcement) will hold or slip again.
- Accessibility and cybersecurity duties tied to selling into the EU rather than being headquartered there, catching non-EU small exporters and web-sellers who may not track EU deadlines at all.
